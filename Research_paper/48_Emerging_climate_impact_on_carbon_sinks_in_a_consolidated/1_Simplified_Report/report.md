#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The Global Carbon Budget has never balanced, and its imbalance had a significant trend. The authors correct four known biases: land-use emissions now include CO₂-driven carbon-density changes, the land sink accounts for real land cover and river export, and the ocean sink accounts for skin temperature and model bias. The land sink falls to 2.7 GtC/yr, the ocean sink rises to 3.1 GtC/yr, and the imbalance drops to −0.1. Climate change has cut the land sink by 23% and added 8.3 ppm to atmospheric CO₂ since 1960.


## 1. Background

Fossil CO₂ emissions keep rising: CO₂ reached 423 ppm in 2024 and human-induced warming 1.36 °C. The 2024 Global Carbon Budget had an imbalance of −0.4 GtC/yr over the last decade and a significant trend of −0.14 GtC/yr per decade, which prevented robust interpretation of sink trends (pp. 3–4).


## 2. Aim of the study

To integrate recent advances in observations and process understanding to resolve long-standing issues in the budget, and then quantify how climate change is affecting the sinks (pp. 3–5).


## 3. What the authors did

Static carbon densities in bookkeeping models were replaced with transient densities from DGVMs; the land sink was corrected for replaced sinks and sources (real land cover) and for the anthropogenic increase in lateral carbon export; the ocean sink was corrected for the cool skin and warm layer in fCO₂ products and a ~10% low bias in ocean models. Extra model runs under constant climate isolated the climate effect (pp. 5–9, Methods).


## 4. Main findings

The revised budget agrees with atmospheric inversions and O₂-based estimates. Climate change reduced the land sink by 0.8 ± 0.9 GtC/yr (−23%) and the ocean sink by 0.18 GtC/yr (−6%); the cumulative loss of 30 ± 6 GtC since 1960 added 8.3 ± 1.4 ppm (8%) to atmospheric CO₂ (pp. 8–9). On land, CO₂ fertilisation gives a 3.6 GtC/yr sink, while climate (0.9) and land use (1.2) are sources, leaving 1.4 GtC/yr. Deforestation drives losses in Africa and Southeast Asia; climate dominates in South America. North America, Europe and China are net sinks from land-use change (pp. 8–10). NOTE The paper gives the revised net land flux as 1.4 ± 1.1 and 1.4 ± 1.2 GtC/yr in different places, and the GCB2024 imbalance as ± 1.4 and ± 1.3.


## 5. Conclusions

1. The consolidated budget has virtually no imbalance and no significant imbalance trend (−0.06 GtC/decade, p = 0.14). 2. Climate change is already reducing the efficiency of the sinks, especially on land. 3. Halting deforestation and limiting warming are needed to stop more of the land biosphere becoming a CO₂ source (pp. 9–10).


## 6. Limitations

- Large interannual variability remains in the imbalance; agreement may reflect compensating errors.
- Degradation, long-term fire effects, ocean small-scale processes and Southern Ocean coverage need work.
- Corrections are within the old uncertainty ranges; global and regional scale only.
