#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Carbon models usually ignore biodiversity. The authors projected plant species loss in 818 ecoregions under two scenarios, converted it to biomass loss with experimental biodiversity-biomass relationships, and applied this to projected carbon maps. Long-term loss of plant diversity could release 7.44–103.14 Pg C under global sustainability and 10.87–145.95 Pg C under fossil-fuelled development (abstract), on top of direct land-use emissions. Losses concentrate in the Amazon, central Africa and Southeast Asia. Protecting and restoring plant diversity, not just forest area, helps climate mitigation.


## 1. Background

Hundreds of experiments show that more diverse plant communities hold more biomass and carbon, through fuller resource use and the presence of highly productive species. Yet Earth System Models use a few plant functional types and nature-based initiatives focus on forest extent, not diversity (pp. 1–2).


## 2. Aim of the study

To estimate the carbon storage lost because of plant biodiversity loss under climate and land-use change scenarios, building on Isbell et al. by accounting for species compositional turnover (p. 2).


## 3. What the authors did

BILBI, fitted with more than 52 million records of over 254,000 plant species at ~1 km², projected species persistence under SSP1/RCP2.6 (global sustainability) and SSP5/RCP8.5 (fossil-fuelled development). Species-area  relationships  gave  species  loss  per  ecoregion;  biodiversity-biomass  slopes  from  the O'Connor et al. meta-analysis gave proportional biomass loss; this was applied to CMIP5 IPSL-CM5A-MR carbon maps (p. 2).

## 5. Conclusions

1. Biodiversity loss can be a strong driver of carbon emissions, creating a self-reinforcing feedback with climate change. 2. Even a Paris-compliant scenario carries large losses, so extra mitigation may be needed and carbon budgets may be overestimated. 3. Restoration should target plant diversity, with the right native species, not just forest extent; non- native monocultures can harm both biodiversity and carbon (pp. 5–7).


## 6. Limitations

- Biodiversity-biomass slopes come from small-scale, short experiments.
- BILBI allows no adaptation or species gains, so losses are pessimistic.
- Losses are long-term equilibria with no exact date.
- Single climate model (IPSL-CM5A-MR), which overestimates circumpolar carbon; only plant diversity considered.
