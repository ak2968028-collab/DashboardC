#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

In a long-term trial at Kanpur, UP (started 2003, soil sampled in 2017), adding pulses to maize–wheat improved porosity, water holding, available nutrients and aggregates. SOC at 0–20 cm was 4.33 g/kg under pigeonpea–wheat vs 3.82 under maize–wheat (from 2.8 at the start), a 10–18% rotation effect. Integrated nutrient management with FYM raised SOC a further 11–18%. Adding mungbean raised maize and wheat yields by 26% and 21% in 2015–16.


## 1. Background

Maize–wheat mono-cropping, tillage and continuous chemical fertiliser have degraded IGP soils. Rice– wheat (9.64 Mha) and maize–wheat (1.83 Mha) dominate the region. Pulses and organic amendments may restore soil properties (p. 1).


## 2. Aim of the study

To evaluate the long-term impact of pulse-based rotations with integrated nutrient management on soil physical and chemical properties, and their links with yield and its sustainability.


## 3. What the authors did

Four rotations (M–W, M–W–Mb, M–W–M–C, P–W) were combined with three fertility levels (control, RDF 120:60:40 for cereals, INM with FYM and residue) in a split-plot design with three replications at IIPR Kanpur. Soil at 0–20 and 20–40 cm was sampled after 14 years (April 2017).

## 5. Conclusions

1. Including pulses improves soil physical and chemical properties over cereal–cereal systems. 2. INM with FYM and residue works best with pulse rotations. 3. Pigeonpea–wheat gave the highest sustainable yield index for wheat.


## 6. Limitations

- One research station; not farmers' fields.
- SOC as concentration; no stocks or sequestration rates.
- No greenhouse gas measurements.
