# Key Highlights: A global meta-analysis on the effects of organic and inorganic fertilization on grasslands and croplands

**Authors:** Ting-Shuai Shi, Scott L. Collins, Kailiang Yu, Josep Peñuelas, Jordi Sardans, Hailing Li, Jian-Sheng Ye
**Journal / Year:** Nature Communications, vol. 15, article 3411 (2024) (2024)
**Official DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47829-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-47829-w)

## Important Findings & Key Takeaways

### 01. Biomass
Organic +56%, inorganic +42% vs unfertilised.

*(Reference: p. 2)*

### 02. Diversity
Only inorganic cut diversity (richness −18%, evenness −6%).

*(Reference: p. 2)*

### 03. SOC
Organic +19% vs unfertilised and +15% vs inorganic; inorganic only +2%.

*(Reference: p. 2)*

### 04. Climate
SOC +32% in cooler areas (grassland +14%); +34% in warmer areas (grassland +62%).

*(Reference: p. 3)*

### 05. Tillage
May explain why warm cropland gains fall with temperature.

*(Reference: p. 5)*

### 06. Rate and start
Gain rises with organic rate; falls with higher initial SOC.

*(Reference: p. 3)*

### 07. Inputs
Use locally sourced, non-contaminated manure.

*(Reference: p. 5)*
