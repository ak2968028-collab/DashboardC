#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The authors used GOSAT satellite methane data with the GEOS-Chem model to estimate 2019 emissions by sector at 1° and for each country. Agriculture and waste gave 263 ± 24 Tg CH₄/yr, fossil fuels 82 ± 12 and wetlands/aquatic 180 ± 10. For India, the satellite estimate of human emissions was 39.5 Tg CH₄/yr against 24.5 in the inventory, mostly from livestock. The authors treat such gaps as a starting point for research, since satellite estimates may have systematic errors.


## 1. Background

Methane is the second most important human greenhouse gas and has a lifetime of about 9 years. The Paris Agreement global stocktake (from 2023) relies on bottom-up inventories, which can carry large uncertainties (pp. 1–2).


## 2. Aim of the study

To show how satellite data can test and update sector and country methane emissions for the global stocktake.


## 3. What the authors did

Optimal estimation turned GOSAT total-column CH₄ into fluxes using GEOS-Chem. A new Bayesian method projected fluxes to sectors at 1° and to countries, with uncertainty and degrees of freedom for signal (DOFS). Prior swapping tested inventories with larger aquatic and fossil emissions.

## 5. Conclusions

1. Satellite estimates can differ substantially (within uncertainty) from inventories. 2. The Bayesian method lets other priors be tested easily. 3. Higher-resolution satellites can cut uncertainty further.


## 6. Limitations

- Possible systematic errors in satellite data and model transport.
- Rice, waste and livestock signals overlap in India.
- 1° resolution and a single year (2019).
