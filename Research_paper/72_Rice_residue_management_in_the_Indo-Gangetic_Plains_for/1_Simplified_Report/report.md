#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

India grows paddy on 43.8 Mha, producing 118.43 Mt of grain and about 165.8 Mt of straw. About 50 Mt of straw is burned each year, releasing about 1.5 Mt of particulates and 150 Mt of CO₂. The review shows that in-situ options (Happy Seeder, SMS, chopper-spreader, plough) and ex-situ uses (biofuel, biochar, power, bales) are profitable and better for soil, but adoption is slow. Uttar Pradesh produces the largest share of India's kharif (19.1%) and rabi (17%) crop biomass.


## 1. Background

Burning is the most common way to clear rice residue because it is simple and cheap, mechanical harvesting has spread, the gap between rice harvest and wheat sowing is short, and straw has few viable uses. About 50 Mt of rice straw is burned each year, nearly half in north-west India in October– November (p. 1).


## 2. Aim of the study

To review how residue burning affects the environment and health, and how in-situ and ex-situ management can cut pollution and improve soil health, crop yield and farm income.


## 3. What the authors did

A narrative review of farmers' practices and perceptions, the claimed benefits of burning (disease and weed control), its harms (pollution, health, soil), in-situ technologies, ex-situ uses and government interventions.

- Farmers mostly remove residue (about 37%), then mulch with the Happy Seeder (31%), rotavator (14%) or super seeder (7.7%) (one survey, p. 5).
- In one Punjab survey, over 90% of farmers agreed the Happy Seeder saves water, labour and fuel and 88% that it cuts air pollution, but only 67.1% agreed it raises short-term yield (p. 13).
- Incorporation or mulching improves soil physical, chemical and biological properties more than removal.
- Retaining rice residue could cut CO₂ by 150 Mt and add 240 Mt C of SOC a year, a cited estimate (Chen et al. 2019) that is not India-specific.

## 5. Conclusions

1. Rice residue burning harms climate, air, health and soil. 2. In-situ management is profitable and improves soil health. 3. Ex-situ uses add value and cut pollution; both should be promoted.


## 6. Limitations

- A review: numbers come from many sources and years.
- Most field data are from Punjab and Haryana, not eastern UP.
- Machine costs and subsidies vary by region and time.
