#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A 2022–23 survey of 1,168 wheat farmers in three Bihar districts compared 12 conventional-tillage and 6 no-till practices using input-based emission factors. No-till wheat had lower input emissions (as low as 1,746 kg CO₂e/ha, NT-2), higher yields (up to 5,268 kg/ha) and positive carbon sustainability index values. Chemical fertilisers made up 74–83% of emissions. However, the paper's headline totals, carbon offsets and CSI values disagree between the abstract, text and tables, so its direction of results is more reliable than its single figures.


## 1. Background

India is the world's second-largest wheat producer. Machinery, diesel, irrigation, fertiliser and plant protection all add to wheat's GHG emissions, so practices need to be judged on both yield and environmental cost. In the survey districts, no-till wheat was promoted under Bihar's Climate Resilient Agriculture programme.


## 2. Aim of the study

To compare conventional-tillage and no-till wheat in Bihar for CO₂-equivalent emissions, energy sources, carbon offset potential and the carbon sustainability index (CSI).


## 3. What the authors did

They interviewed 1,168 farmers in Begusarai (Zone I, 423), Madhepura (Zone II, 395) and Buxar (Zone III, 350) in 2022–23. Practices were grouped into CP-1 to CP-12 (rotavator or disc tillage, broadcast sowing) and NT-1 to NT-6 (zero-till seed-cum-fertiliser drill), differing in weed pressure and harvest method.

Inputs were multiplied by emission factors (Table 3) within a farm-gate boundary. Carbon offset was based on collected straw; CSI = (offset − emission) ÷ emission.


## 4. Main findings

- Fertilisers were the main source (74–83% of emissions), followed by diesel; indirect energy made up 81–88% and non-renewable energy 91–93%.
- No-till practices had the lowest input emissions (NT-2: 1,745.66 kg CO₂e/ha).
- Yield ranged from 3,575 kg/ha (CP-1) to 5,268 kg/ha (NT-3 and NT-6); carbon footprint from about 0.50 to 0.82 kg CO₂e/kg grain, highest in CP-1.

## 5. Conclusions

1. Conventional tillage wheat is more emission-intensive, mainly because of fertiliser and diesel use. 2. No-till wheat lowers emissions, raises yield and improves carbon sustainability. 3. Policymakers should support no-till and better nutrient management in Bihar.


## 6. Limitations

- One season of survey (recall) data; no field measurements.
- Input-based only: soil carbon change and field N₂O are not fully measured.
- Many internal inconsistencies in totals and indices.
- Bihar, not Uttar Pradesh; practice variants mix several factors.
