#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A 2018–19 survey of 240 farmers in 24 villages of Anantnag, Kashmir, compared four agroforestry systems. Horti-silvipasture (apple + poplar + grasses) had the most biomass (66.55 t/ha) and carbon (29.94 t C/ha), followed by agri-horti-silviculture (22.56), horti-agriculture (16.28) and boundary plantation (15.67 t C/ha). Carbon was taken as 0.45 × biomass and CO₂e as C × 3.67, so the 'sequestration potential' is a standing stock, not a yearly rate.


## 1. Background

With India self-sufficient in food, attention is turning to ecological problems and shortages of fuel, fodder and jobs. Agroforestry can serve as a carbon sink while supporting food, income and habitat. A cited study puts plantation productivity at 3.2 Mg/ha/yr vs 1.1 for natural forest (Lal and Singh, 2000).


## 2. Aim of the study

To assess biomass, carbon stock and carbon sequestration potential of prominent agroforestry systems in south Kashmir for climate change mitigation.


## 3. What the authors did

Multistage random sampling chose six tehsils, one block each, four villages per block and ten farmers per village (240 farmers, 25% intensity). Tree biomass came from diameter and height equations; fruit trees, crops and grasses were estimated separately. Carbon = 0.45 × biomass and CO₂e = C × 3.67.

## 5. Conclusions

1. Agroforestry systems differ significantly in biomass and carbon. 2. Horti-silvipasture stores the most carbon; boundary plantation the least. 3. Agroforestry supports climate mitigation and farm income.


## 6. Limitations

- Temperate, apple-based Kashmir systems; one survey year.
- Default 0.45 carbon fraction; no soil carbon measured.
- 'Sequestration potential' is a stock, not an annual rate.
