## Important Findings & Key Takeaways

### 01. Global MOC stock
899 Pg C to 1 m (448 topsoil, 451 subsoil), 66% and 70% of soil carbon in surface and deeper layers.

*(Reference: pp. 1, 4)*

### 02. Capacity
Mineralogical capacity 4596 Pg C to 1 m; soils are only 42% (topsoil) and 21% (subsoil) saturated.

*(Reference: p. 5)*

### 03. Mineral type matters
MOCmax 86 ± 9 (high-activity) vs 48 ± 6 mg C/g (low-activity) clay + silt.

*(Reference: pp. 2–3, Fig. 1)*

### 04. Farm soils are emptier
Agricultural soils 31% saturated vs 46% in natural ecosystems; deep soils 19% vs surface 43%.

*(Reference: p. 3, Fig. 2)*

### 05. Cropland deficit
184 Pg C (topsoil) and 509 Pg C (subsoil).

*(Reference: p. 5)*

### 06. Realistic gain
Croplands at natural saturation would gain about 104 Pg C (62 topsoil + 42 subsoil).

*(Reference: p. 5)*

### 07. Emptiest soils gain fastest
Across 103 accrual measurements, rates were 3× higher at <10% than at 50% saturation.

*(Reference: p. 6, Fig. 4d)*

### 08. India is a hotspot
Extensive cropping regions such as the Midwestern US and India are sequestration hotspots.

*(Reference: p. 6)*

### 09. Rates slow down
Accrual falls as soils fill; >1 Pg C/yr over all croplands is possible only for a time.

*(Reference: p. 6)*

### 10. Warming risk
MOC declines with temperature; unsaturated soils are more temperature-sensitive.

*(Reference: p. 4, Fig. 3)*
