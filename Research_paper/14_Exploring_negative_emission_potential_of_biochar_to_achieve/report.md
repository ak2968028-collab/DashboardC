#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The authors mapped China's biomass residues on a 0.5° grid and modelled biochar made at 550 °C with syngas co-production. In the sustainable scenario, which keeps residues needed for fodder and ecology, biochar could remove up to 0.92 Gt CO₂ a year at an average net cost of about 90 $/t CO₂. That covers the negative emission demand in most scenarios for China's 2060 neutrality target. Syngas sales cover 32–57% of costs, yield gains only 1–23%. Guangxi, Henan and Shandong are the priority pilot provinces.


## 1. Background

Human activity caused 0.9–1.3 °C of warming in 2010–2019 compared with pre-industrial times. For China to reach carbon neutrality, integrated assessment model scenarios expect negative emission technologies (NETs) to remove 0.01–2.91 Gt CO₂ per year between 2050 and 2060 (p. 1). BECCS can cost more than 100 $ and DACCS more than 200 $ per tonne of CO₂, and both face resource and geological limits; BECCS also competes with crops for land and water. Biochar is an established technology, but its national potential, cost and best locations in China were uncertain (p. 1).


## 2. Aim of the study

To estimate, on a map grid, the negative emission potential, economics and priority deployment sites of biochar from several feedstocks in China, and to test whether biochar could meet China's negative emission needs for carbon neutrality by 2060 (pp. 1–2).


## 3. What the authors did

They estimated 16 types of crop residue, 10 types of forestry residue, grass residues and energy crops (such as sweet sorghum and miscanthus) on a 0.5° × 0.5° grid under three scenarios (pp. 2–4, 7–8). Livestock manure was left out (p. 7).

## 4. Main findings

- Negative emission potential is 1.29 Gt CO₂/yr (maximum theoretical), 0.92 Gt CO₂/yr (sustainable) and 0.43 Gt CO₂/yr (current) (p. 4).
- The median NET demand for China is 1.43 Gt CO₂/yr in 2050 or 2060; with a managed-forest sink of 0.63 Gt CO₂/yr, biochar could meet demand in most scenarios without immature BECCS or DACCS (p. 4).
- Avoided emissions (syngas replacing fossil fuel, lower soil GHG) are about 1.5 times the removal (p. 4).
- The average net cost is about 90 $/t CO₂: 60–96 for agricultural and forestry residues and 101–144 for energy crops and grass residues. BECCS is typically 30–400 and DACCS 30–1000 $/t CO₂ (pp. 4–5).
- Syngas sales offset 32–57% of total costs; yield co-benefits offset only 1–23%. A cited meta-analysis puts biochar's yield gain at about 10% in China against 35% globally (p. 4).
- Agricultural-residue biochar can give a net benefit in some places, driven by yield gains on high-yield cereals in Shandong and Henan and sugarcane in Guangxi; the cost map scale runs down to −52 $/t CO₂ (pp. 5, 7, Fig. 4b).
- Guangxi, Henan and Shandong are priority pilot provinces; pilots could start with agroforestry residues (p. 6).
- Monte Carlo: mean potential 1.07 Gt CO₂/yr (0.68–1.46) and mean cost 92 $/t CO₂ (−13 to 197) (p. 7).

## 5. Conclusions

Biochar is a promising near-term NET for China that should be in the climate mitigation toolbox. Deployment should target regions with high potential and low cost, starting with pilots in Guangxi, Henan and Shandong. Biochar is in the IPCC 2019 Refinement, but local accounting methods are still needed, and the authors call for its inclusion in national emission trading and Article 6 of the Paris Agreement (pp. 6–7).


## 6. Limitations

- Not all biomass types are covered; livestock manure was excluded (p. 7).
- The co-production technology does not maximise biochar yield (p. 7).
- Technology learning, economies of scale and changing carbon prices are not modelled (p. 7).
- Land-use change, food competition and biodiversity trade-offs are not fully addressed (p. 7).
- Results use Chinese biomass, soil and price data.
CORRECTION TO THE EARLIER NOTES The −52 $/t CO₂ figure is the lowest value on the cost map scale for agricultural residues (Fig. 4b), not a reported value for Henan. The paper only says net benefits occur in some places in Shandong, Henan and Guangxi.
