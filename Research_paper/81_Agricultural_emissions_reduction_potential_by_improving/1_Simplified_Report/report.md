#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Using 2017–18 plot data for about 10,700 rice and 5,200 wheat plots across India, the authors estimated emissions with CCAFS-MOT and efficiency with a stochastic frontier. Mean technical efficiency was 0.83 in rice and 0.77 in wheat. Higher efficiency lowered emissions per kg of grain but not total emissions per hectare. Many smallholders were both efficient and low-emitting. In Uttar Pradesh, efficiency was 0.807 for rice and 0.780 for wheat.


## 1. Background

Agricultural policy tends to push new technology rather than better use of existing technology. India ranks third globally in agricultural emissions, and small and marginal farmers are 86% of all farmers (p. 2).


## 2. Aim of the study

To assess how technical efficiency relates to GHG emissions, testing whether better efficiency reduces crop emissions.


## 3. What the authors did

Plot-level data from the 2017–18 Cost of Cultivation Survey (18 rice and 13 wheat states) were combined with soil, climate and water-regime data. CCAFS-MOT estimated GWP and emission intensity; a translog stochastic frontier estimated efficiency and its drivers.

## 5. Conclusions

1. Better use of existing technology can cut emissions per unit of food. 2. Total emissions per hectare may not fall as efficiency rises. 3. Farming practices, not farm size, mainly drive efficiency and emissions.


## 6. Limitations

- Emissions are modelled with CCAFS-MOT, not measured.
- Cross-sectional, one survey year (2017–18).
- Farm-size results (Table 4) are given only as an image.
