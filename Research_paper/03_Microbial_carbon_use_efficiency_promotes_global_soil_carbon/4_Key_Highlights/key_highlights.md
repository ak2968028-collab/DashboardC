## Important Findings & Key Takeaways

### 01. CUE dominates
Microbial CUE is at least four times as important as plant inputs, decomposition, vertical transport and three other components for global SOC storage.

*(Reference: pp. 981, 984)*

### 02. Positive link
log₁₀(SOC) = 1.16 + 0.76 × CUE, explaining 54.7% of variation in 132 measurements from 16 studies.

*(Reference: p. 982, Fig. 2a)*

### 03. Small change, big effect
+10% global SOC (136 PgC) needs +2.0% CUE (0.28 → 0.29), versus –8.4% decomposition or +11% environmental modifiers.

*(Reference: p. 984, Fig. 4b)*

### 04. Uniform CUE fails
One global CUE value shifts SOC by 812 PgC (of 1,358 PgC) and cuts explained variation from 53% to 15%.

*(Reference: p. 984, Fig. 4a)*

### 05. Inputs matter little
Changes in carbon inputs, allocation and vertical transport only marginally affect global SOC.

*(Reference: p. 984)*

### 06. Tropics vs boreal
Tropical soils get more plant carbon but hold less SOC because of low CUE and fast decomposition.

*(Reference: pp. 983–984)*

### 07. Soil structure
Bulk density, texture and porosity explain CUE better than climate, soil chemistry or vegetation.

*(Reference: p. 984)*

### 08. Measured CUE
Mean 0.33 with both C (¹³C/¹⁴C) and O (¹⁸O) isotope methods.

### 09. Data gaps and assumptions
Few tropical, arid and clay-soil sites; steady state assumed; sensitivity to model structure not yet tested.

*(Reference: p. 984; Methods)*
