#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The authors surveyed 250 marginal and small farm households in western Tamil Nadu, grouped them into four farm types by PCA and cluster analysis, and used the Cool Farm Tool on one representative farm per type. Cash crop and plantation farms had the highest crop-side emissions (residue burning, fertiliser production and application), cereal farms the lowest, and the livestock farm the highest enteric (10,500 kg CO₂e/yr) and manure emissions. CFT also estimated soil carbon sequestration (6,071–15,905 kg CO₂e/yr). Mitigation must fit each farm type.


## 1. Background

Agriculture contributes 14–17% of GHG emissions. Direct measurement is too costly for countries dominated by marginal and small farms, which are very diverse; farm typologies with calculators such as CFT are a practical alternative (pp. 1–2).


## 2. Aim of the study

To identify farm types and quantify the GHG balance of each among marginal and small households in western Tamil Nadu using the Cool Farm Tool (p. 2).


## 3. What the authors did

A 2022–23 questionnaire (baseline 2020–21) covered 250 households in Coimbatore, Tiruppur and Erode. PCA gave three components (56.7%, 22.3%, 12.5% of variation) and Ward clustering four types. One representative farm per type was surveyed in depth in February–April 2024 and run through CFT,

which uses IPCC Tier 1–2 equations and counts soil carbon sequestration as a sink. Trees, electricity, irrigation and post-harvest processing were not counted (pp. 2–3).

## 5. Conclusions

1. GHG emissions differ significantly across the four farm types. 2. Plantation and cash crop farms have higher crop emissions; the livestock farm has higher livestock emissions. 3. Mitigation should be tailored to farm type, and CFT gives a whole-farm picture (p. 5).


## 6. Limitations

- One representative farm per type; totals per farm, not per hectare.
- Generic CFT factors; trees, electricity and irrigation excluded.
- Some text values differ from Tables 1–2 (type 4 n = 24 vs 26; type 1 fertiliser 414 vs 546).
- Western Tamil Nadu crops differ from the Varuna basin's rice-wheat.
