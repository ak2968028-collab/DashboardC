# Key Highlights: Diversifying crop rotation increases food production, reduces net greenhouse gas emissions and improves soil health

**Authors:** Xiaolin Yang, Jinran Xiong, Taisheng Du, Xiaotang Ju, Yantai Gan, Sien Li, Longlong Xia, Yanjun Shen, Steven Pacenka, Tammo S. Steenhuis, Kadambot H. M. Siddique, Shaozhong Kang, Klaus Butterbach-Bahl
**Journal / Year:** Nature Communications, vol. 15, article 198 (2024) (2023)
**Official DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-44464-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-44464-9)

## Important Findings & Key Takeaways

### 01. Higher yields
Sweet potato rotation: equivalent yield +38%, net income +60%; wheat–maize after non-cereals +26–32% grain.

*(Reference: pp. 2–3)*

### 02. All-cereal rotation loses
Spring maize rotation: equivalent yield –16%, protein –23%.

*(Reference: p. 3)*

### 03. Less N, less N₂O
Legume rotations used 37% less fertiliser; N₂O fell 30%, 42% and 49% (peanut, soybean, sweet potato).

*(Reference: pp. 3, 8)*

### 04. GWP of soil gases
Wheat–maize 3764 kg CO₂-eq/ha/yr; 32–51% lower with legumes or sweet potato, 19–22% with cereal rotations.

*(Reference: p. 3)*

### 05. Indirect emissions
Wheat–maize 8802 kg CO₂-eq/ha/yr from inputs and irrigation; 34–41% lower in four diversified rotations.

*(Reference: p. 3)*

### 06. Net GHG
Wheat–maize 10,025 kg CO₂-eq/ha/yr; sweet potato, soybean and peanut rotations –83%, –90%, –92%.

*(Reference: p. 4)*

### 07. Soil carbon
Peanut 2.03, soybean 1.91, sweet potato 1.44 t C/ha/yr (0–90 cm) vs 0.21–0.69 for the other rotations.

*(Reference: p. 3, Fig. 3e)*

### 08. Soil health
Scores 59.5, 56.8 and 52.9 vs 33.2 for wheat–maize (+41–59%); microbial diversity +7–10%.

*(Reference: pp. 4–5)*

### 09. Regional benefit
106.8 Mt CO₂-eq/yr less (5.6% of China's food-system GHG); +20% farmer income; +32% wheat–maize yield.

*(Reference: p. 5)*
