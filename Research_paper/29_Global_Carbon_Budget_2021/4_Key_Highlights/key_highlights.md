## Important Findings & Key Takeaways

### 01. Emissions in the COVID year
Fossil CO₂ 9.3 ± 0.5 GtC/yr (incl. cement carbonation sink) plus land-use change 0.9 ± 0.7 = 10.2 ± 0.8 GtC/yr.

### 02. Where it went
Atmosphere 5.0 ± 0.2, ocean 3.0 ± 0.4, land 2.9 ± 1 GtC/yr; imbalance −0.8 GtC/yr.

### 03. Concentration
CO₂ averaged 412.45 ppm in 2020 and was set to reach 414.7 ppm in 2021, 50% above pre-industrial.

*(Reference: pp. 1919–1920)*

### 04. Decade shares
In 2011–2020 the atmosphere kept 47%, the ocean took 26% and the land 29% of emissions.

*(Reference: p. 1920)*

### 05. 2021 rebound
Fossil CO₂ +4.8% (4.2–5.4%) to 9.9 GtC, about the 2019 level; China +5.7% and India +3.2% above 2019.

*(Reference: p. 1920)*

### 06. Countries
Last 10 years: India +3.9%/yr, China +1.0%, EU27 −1.9%, USA −1.1%.

*(Reference: p. 1932)*

### 07. Historical responsibility
1850–2020 fossil CO₂: USA 110 GtC (25%), EU 80 GtC (18%), China 60 GtC (14%).

*(Reference: p. 1932)*

### 08. Small net, large gross
Gross emissions 3.8 minus gross removals 2.7 = net 1.1 ± 0.7 GtC/yr (2011–2020).

*(Reference: p. 1920)*

### 09. Link to national inventories
First reconciliation: once part of the natural sink on managed land is counted, the two approaches agree.

*(Reference: pp. 1919–1920)*

### 10. Years left
From January 2022: 120 GtC for 1.5 °C, 210 GtC for 1.7 °C, 350 GtC for 2 °C (≈ 11, 20 and 32 years).

*(Reference: p. 1951)*

### 11. Pace needed for net zero 2050
Cut about 0.4 GtC (1.4 GtCO₂) per year, like the 2020 drop.

*(Reference: p. 1951)*

### 12. Three gaps
Land-use change emissions, northern extra-tropical land flux and the recent ocean sink trend (a factor of 3 between methods).
