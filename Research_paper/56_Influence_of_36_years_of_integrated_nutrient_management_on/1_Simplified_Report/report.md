#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

In a rice-wheat experiment running since 1984 at Sabour, Bihar, replacing half the rice nitrogen with farmyard manure (and giving full fertiliser to wheat, T6) gave the highest soil carbon stock (16.85 Mg/ha), a sequestration rate of 0.22 Mg C/ha/yr and the highest wheat yield (4.29 Mg/ha, +24.7% over fertiliser alone). Replacing a quarter of the N with FYM (T7) gave the lowest modelled emission intensity, 206.6 kg CO₂-eq per tonne of grain. Plots with no inputs lost soil carbon.


## 1. Background

Wheat is grown on about 32 Mha in India. Years of intensive farming and unbalanced fertiliser use have left soils short of nutrients and low in organic matter; the paper cites an annual NPK requirement of 28 Mt against 18 Mt supplied, a negative balance of 10 Mt (p. 2). Integrated nutrient management (INM) combines chemical fertilisers with organic sources such as farmyard manure (FYM), crop straw and green manure. Long-term fertiliser experiments are the best way to see how such practices change soil organic carbon (SOC) and greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, since these change slowly.


## 2. Aim of the study

To test how 36 years of different INM combinations affect SOC sequestration, the environmental footprint (GHG emission intensity) and wheat productivity in a rice-wheat system of the eastern Indo- Gangetic Plains (EIGP).


## 3. What the authors did

The experiment began in  1984 at the Bihar Agricultural University research farm, Sabour (humid subtropical; average rainfall 1280 mm, 75–80% from mid-June to mid-October). It has 11 treatments in a randomised block design with three replications (plots 8.10 × 4.20 m). ## 4. Main findings


### 4.1 Soil carbon

SOC stocks after 36 rice-wheat cycles ranged from 8.05 Mg/ha (control) to 16.85 Mg/ha (T6), against an initial stock of 10.28 Mg/ha. T6 and T8 raised the SOC stock by 36.4% and 29.9% over full fertiliser (T5). Carbon sequestered ranged from −2.24 to 8.07 Mg/ha, and the sequestration rate from −0.06 to 0.22 Mg C/ha/yr. Plots with no inputs (T1) or only 50% RDF to rice (T2, T3) lost organic carbon. FYM was the most effective organic source. The carbon pool index ranged from 1.06 to 2.40 (highest in T6), and bulk density fell from 1.49 to 1.37 Mg/m³ under T6.

### 4.3 Emission intensity

Combined organic-inorganic treatments had lower emission intensity than fertiliser alone. The lowest was 206.6 kg CO₂-eq/Mg grain (T7); the highest were the control (328.1, because of its low yield) and full RDF (286.9). Emission intensity was negatively correlated with yield (r = −0.667).


## 5. Conclusions

1. Organic manure with balanced chemical fertiliser significantly increased SOC sequestration and yield and reduced emission intensity. 2. FYM was more effective than wheat straw or green manure. 3. Carbon management in this system is only just above the threshold level and must be increased to restore soil health and productivity.


## 6. Limitations

- One research station and one measured wheat season (2020–21, an unusually dry season with 0.4 mm rain).
- GHG emissions were estimated with a model (CCAFS-MOT), not measured in the field.
- SOC was measured only in the top 15 cm; FYM availability on farms may limit adoption at these rates.
- The paper has small internal inconsistencies (46.4% vs 44.9% SOC gain; initial SOC 10.30 vs 10.28 Mg/ha; site longitude printed as 78°E).
