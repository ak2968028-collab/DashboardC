## Important Findings & Key Takeaways

### 01. Long-term trial in Bihar
Started in 1984 at Sabour, eastern IGP; 11 treatments, 3 replications; wheat measured in 2020–21.

*(Reference: pp. 2–4)*

### 02. Organic sources
FYM, wheat straw or Sesbania green manure replaced 25% or 50% of rice nitrogen.

### 03. FYM built the most carbon
T6 (50% N as FYM in rice) reached an SOC stock of 16.85 Mg/ha and sequestered 8.07 Mg C/ha at 0.22 Mg C/ha/yr.

*(Reference: pp. 5–6, Fig. 3–4)*

### 04. No inputs depletes SOC
The control fell from 10.28 to 8.05 Mg/ha (−0.06 Mg C/ha/yr); 50% RDF alone also lost carbon.

*(Reference: pp. 5–6)*

### 05. Carbon pool index
Ranged from 1.06 (control) to 2.40 (T6).

### 06. Highest yield
T6 gave 4.29 Mg/ha, +24.7% over full fertiliser (3.44 Mg/ha); control 0.96 Mg/ha.

### 07. Better nutrient-use efficiency
INM raised agronomic efficiency and partial factor productivity of N, P and K; highest AE in T7.

*(Reference: p. 6, Fig. 5–7)*

### 08. Lowest emission intensity
206.6 kg CO₂-eq/t grain with 25% N as FYM (T7), vs 286.9 for full fertiliser and 328.1 for the control.

*(Reference: p. 6, Fig. 8)*

### 09. Modelled, not measured
Emissions estimated with CCAFS-MOT (GWP: CH₄ 34, N₂O 298).

*(Reference: p. 5)*

### 10. Two figures for the SOC gain
Abstract: +46.4%; Results and Table 3: +44.9% organic carbon over full fertiliser (+36.4% in SOC stock).

*(Reference: pp. 1, 5–6)*
