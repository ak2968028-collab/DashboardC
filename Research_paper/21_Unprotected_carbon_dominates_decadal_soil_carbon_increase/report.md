#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

From 7,219 soil samples on six continents (2000–2022), particulate organic carbon (POC) rose by 21.8% while mineral-associated carbon (MAOC) fell by 5.3% (not significant), giving an 11.0% rise in total SOC and a 29.1% rise in the POC:MAOC ratio. Soil carbon is growing but becoming less stable. Planted forests and grazed grasslands lost POC, and Asian croplands lost POC while North American croplands gained it. The authors call for strategies that protect POC and help convert it to MAOC.


## 1. Background

Soil organic carbon (SOC) splits into particulate organic carbon (POC), which comes mainly from plants, is easy for microbes to reach and lasts years to decades, and mineral-associated organic carbon (MAOC), which comes mainly from microbial by-products bound to clay and silt and lasts decades to centuries (p. 1). Global SOC storage appears to have risen, but how the two fractions changed over time was unquantified (p. 1).


## 2. Aim of the study

To estimate global trends in POC and MAOC from 2000 to 2022 and to test how human disturbance and land management (planted forests, grazing, cropland practice) affect them (p. 2).


## 3. What the authors did

The authors screened 1,826 articles, kept 456, and added data from databases such as ESDAC and SCaRP (p. 5). Only field samples from 0–40 cm depth separated with standard physical fractionation were used: POC was the light (< 1.6–1.85 g/cm³) or coarse (> 20–63 μm) fraction and MAOC the rest (pp. 5–6). This gave 7,219 samples: 3,614 cropland (1,533 Asia, 736 North America), 2,130 forest and 1,475 grassland (p. 6). Linear mixed-effects models estimated trends over sampling year, with depth, continent and land type as random effects weighted by land area (p. 6).

## 4. Main findings

- POC was 27.4% and MAOC 70.0% of total SOC on average (p. 2).
- From 2000 to 2022 POC rose by 21.8% (P < 0.001), MAOC fell by 5.3% (P = 0.163, not significant) and total SOC rose by 11.0% (P = 0.003) (p. 2).
- The POC:MAOC ratio rose by 29.1% (p. 1), signalling falling SOC stability.
- POC fell in planted forests (P = 0.015) and grazed grasslands (P = 0.003) and rose in ungrazed grasslands (P < 0.001) (pp. 2–3).
- Across all croplands POC was stable (P = 0.870), but it fell in Asia and rose in North America (P < 0.001 for both); MAOC followed the same pattern (p. 3).
- MAOC rose in grazed grasslands, but total SOC did not change because POC fell (p. 4).

## 5. Conclusions

The global SOC increase is mainly driven by unprotected POC, so SOC stability is decreasing under climate change and human activity (p. 1). Maintaining soil carbon sinks needs strategies focused on POC, such as reducing grazing intensity, protecting forests and conservation agriculture, while helping POC convert to MAOC through less disturbance, deep-rooted plants and nitrogen-rich inputs (pp. 4–5).


## 6. Limitations

- Trends compare different sites sampled in different years rather than repeat sampling (thesis author's assessment; the paper does not discuss this).
- Only surface soils (0–40 cm) were included (p. 5).
- Results for Asian croplands are pooled; there is no South Asian or Indo-Gangetic result.
- Sample size varies across years; random-subsample checks did not change the conclusions (p. 6). CORRECTION TO THE EARLIER NOTES The earlier notes said fractionation methods may differ between labs. The paper used only standard methods and reports that the choice of method does not alter its conclusions (p. 6, Suppl. Table 7).
