#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Using Landsat images from 1991, 2001, 2011 and 2022 classified with random forest in Google Earth Engine, the authors mapped seven land classes in the Varanasi City Development Region (673 km²). Built-up land rose 507.8% (29.97 to 182.16 km², about six times), with growth speeding up each decade. Agricultural land fell by a net 91.8 km² (72.9% to 59.3% of the area), and 114.8 km² of farmland was converted to built-up land. Overall map accuracy was 86.8–92.8%.


## 1. Background

Economic development and population growth drive land use and land cover (LULC) change. Varanasi, between the Varuna and Assi rivers, is one of the world's oldest cities and a growing religious and service centre, so its surrounding farmland and green cover are under pressure.


## 2. Aim of the study

To analyse the pattern, dynamics and transformation of LULC in the Varanasi City Development Region (VCDR) for 1991, 2001, 2011 and 2022 using Google Earth Engine and machine learning.


## 3. What the authors did

May images from Landsat-5 TM (1991, 2001, 2011) and Landsat-9 OLI-2 (2022) at 30 m were classified into seven classes with a random forest classifier in Google Earth Engine, using stratified training polygons checked against ESRI and NRSC land cover. Change matrices were produced in SAGA GIS. Accuracy was assessed with 735 reference points per year (105 per class), using user, producer and overall accuracy and the Kappa coefficient.

- Built-up land grew by 37.4 km² (1991–2001), 43.38 km² (2001–2011) and 71.41 km² (2011–2022).
- Of the 2022 built-up area, 114.8 km² was farmland, 14.81 km² barren land and 12.93 km² vegetation cover in 1991 (Table 14).
- Vegetation cover declined to 2011 but recovered slightly by 2022 (30.4 to 34.2 km²).
- Growth was concentrated in and around the municipal area and along roads and railways.
- Overall accuracy: 91.83% (1991), 87.89% (2001), 86.80% (2011), 92.79% (2022); Kappa 0.846–0.916. NOTE ON '507.8%' A 507.8% increase means the 2022 built-up area is about 6.1 times the 1991 area, not five times. The 114.8 km² figure is a gross transfer of farmland to built-up; the net loss of farmland was 91.8 km² because some other land became farmland.

## 5. Conclusions

1. Built-up land in VCDR expanded rapidly, mostly at the expense of fertile farmland. 2. This trend is unsustainable and risks food scarcity, environmental degradation and lower quality of urban life. 3. The results give planners a scientific basis for sustainable land use planning in VCDR and other cities.


## 6. Limitations

- Covers the city development region, not the full Varuna basin.
- 30 m resolution misses scattered trees and small plots.
- Only four dates, all in May.
- No carbon stock or emission estimates.
