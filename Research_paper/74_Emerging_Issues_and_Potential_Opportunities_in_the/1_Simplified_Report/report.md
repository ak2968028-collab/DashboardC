#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The rice–wheat system (9.2 Mha in India) is stagnating in north-west India because of soil decline, groundwater fall (8.91 m in Punjab, 2000–2019), labour shortage, residue burning (11.3 Mt of paddy straw in Punjab and Haryana) and greenhouse gases. The review shows that dry direct-seeded rice saves 11–18% water and 11–66% labour, and cited Punjab data show its methane at 0.1–0.3 vs 0.8–1.9 t CO₂e/ha for puddled rice. Adoption needs awareness, capacity building and supporting policy.


## 1. Background

Rice–wheat covers 9.2 Mha in India, mainly in Punjab, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh. Rice is puddled and transplanted, wheat follows after intensive tillage, and the short gap between them delays wheat: sowing after 15 November loses about 32 kg/ha a day (p. 4). Agriculture causes about 16% of India's GHG emissions; methane from livestock (38.9%) and rice (36.9%) dominates (p. 2).


## 2. Aim of the study

To summarise the status and challenges of the rice–wheat system in north-west India and review precision and sustainable intensification options for productivity, profitability and sustainability.


## 3. What the authors did

A narrative review of studies and official statistics on soil health, groundwater, residue burning, costs, labour, emissions and weeds, and on direct-seeded rice, zero tillage, residue retention, diversification and nutrient management.

- A 7-year Haryana study found DDSR–zero-till wheat cost USD 448/ha vs USD 555/ha for the conventional system.
- Satellite data show fire events in UP fell 24% in 2018 vs 2017 and 32% vs 2016. CORRECTION TO EARLIER NOTES The '>90% less methane and 20% lower GWP' applies to dry seeding of rice in moist soil, not to conservation practices in general. The review's own cited data give about one-third lower GWP (1.94 vs 2.91 t/ha) and 82–87% lower methane (Gupta et al. 2016).

## 5. Conclusions

1. The rice–wheat system faces soil, water, air and climate problems. 2. Many intensification technologies cut irrigation, labour, tillage and straw burning. 3. Awareness, capacity building and matching policy must come first for adoption.


## 6. Limitations

- A narrative review: values come from different studies, years and places.
- Focus on mechanised Punjab and Haryana, not eastern UP.
- Methane and GWP reductions are cited, not measured by the authors.
