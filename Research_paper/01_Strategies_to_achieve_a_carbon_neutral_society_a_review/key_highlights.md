## Important Findings & Key Takeaways

### 01. CO₂ is far above pre-industrial levels
Atmospheric CO₂ rose from about 285 ppm (around 1850) to about 419 ppm (2022).

*(Reference: p. 2278)*

### 02. Warming is already above 1 °C
The UK Met Office estimates 1.09 °C of warming over 1850–2022 (range 0.97–1.21 °C).

*(Reference: p. 2278)*

### 03. Few countries have reached neutrality
Only 4.5% of 198 countries have reached carbon neutrality; 60.6% (120 countries) aim for 2050–2070, and most target dates fall after 2050. India's target is 2070.

*(Reference: pp. 2281–2290, Table 1)*

### 04. COP26: coal, cars, cash and trees
Phase out coal, move to electric transport, deliver the US$100 billion a year pledged to developing countries, and protect forests.

*(Reference: pp. 2279–2280)*

### 05. Mapping tools
Input–output models, gridded spatial systems, GIS maps, LiDAR and LMDI-I map direct and indirect emissions.

*(Reference: pp. 2292–2296, Table 2)*

### 06. Three sectors, nearly 80% of CO₂
In a Chinese input–output study, power and heat supply, petroleum processing and coking, and metal mining caused nearly 80% of CO₂ emissions.

*(Reference: p. 2293 (Bai et al. 2018))*

### 07. Two routes to net zero
Cut emissions (policy plus sector technologies) and remove CO₂ (negative emission technologies and nature-based solutions).

*(Reference: p. 2296)*

### 08. Carbon pricing works
China's carbon trading (2008–2018 study) cut emissions sharply in several provinces; carbon-tax incentives can raise airline fuel efficiency.

*(Reference: p. 2297)*

### 09. Biogas to biomethane
Biomethane use in EU CNG and LNG vehicles is expected to reach 30 billion m³/yr by 2030.

*(Reference: p. 2300 (Prussi et al. 2019))*

### 10. Low-carbon farming is a large sink
In South America it could store 8.24 PgC (2016–2050) and add 17.6 Mt/yr of food and 1.6 Mt/yr of meat.

*(Reference: p. 2300 (Sa et al. 2017))*

### 11. Which practices matter most
Pasture restoration 31%, crop-livestock-forestry integration 25.6%, no-till 24.3%, forestation 12.8%, biological N fixation 4.2%, industrial organic waste recycling 2%.

*(Reference: p. 2300)*

### 12. Diet and food waste
An EU study found that a more vegetarian diet, less meat and less food waste mitigate climate change.

*(Reference: p. 2300 (Strapasson et al. 2020))*

### 13. Cheap and costly removal options
In Scotland, enhanced weathering cost the most (US$25–1600 per t CO₂); BECCS and forestation cost less; biochar and soil carbon sequestration could be cost-effective.

*(Reference: p. 2301 (Alcalde et al. 2018))*

### 14. Combine removal methods
A BECCS + soil carbon + enhanced weathering mix could cut 8.3–36.8 Mt CO₂, up to 89.8% of Scotland's annual emissions.

*(Reference: p. 2301)*

### 15. Start with energy
The first step is to shift from fossil fuels to renewables and low-carbon technologies, then electrify energy services and raise efficiency.

*(Reference: pp. 2305–2306)*
