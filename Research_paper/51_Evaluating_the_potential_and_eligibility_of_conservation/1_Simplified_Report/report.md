#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The authors test whether conservation agriculture (CA) in the wheat season can earn carbon credits in Punjab and Bihar under Verra's VM0042. CA cuts 1.23–1.97 t CO₂e/ha, worth USD 18–30/ha to farmers at USD 25 a credit, with no significant yield penalty. Punjab's burning ban and the fact that most farmers already do not burn make 'no burning' ineligible there, dropping farmer income to USD 16/ha; credit prices need to rise ~60%. Zero tillage of wheat (both states) and less N overuse (Punjab) pass all tests.


## 1. Background

Agrifood systems cause nearly a third of global GHG emissions. Carbon credits (1 credit = 1 t CO₂e) can reward farmers, but Verra's VM0042 v2.0 (active since 30 May 2023) sets strict eligibility rules. Emissions from residue burning in India rose 75% from 2011 to 2020 (pp. 1–2).


## 2. Aim of the study

To evaluate the potential and eligibility of wheat-season CA practices for carbon credits in Punjab and Bihar under VM0042 (p. 1).


## 3. What the authors did

CIMMYT  household  surveys  (Punjab:  1,021  households  in  52  villages;  Bihar:  1,003  households, interviewed 2021) gave 1,021 and 1,002 plots. Emissions were estimated with an R version of CCAFS- MOT, and adopters were compared with non-adopters by propensity score matching. Sentinel-2 mapped burned area (2022) and zero tillage (pp. 9–10).

- Additionality: not required by law, and baseline adoption below 20% of area.
- Yield penalty: no more than 5% yield loss.
- Quantitative adjustment: fertiliser cut above 5% (p. 1).

CORRECTION TO THE OLD NOTES The 265 kg CO₂e/ha figure is the cut from zero tillage (measured in Punjab, assumed for Bihar), not from not burning residue. Not burning saves 618 (Bihar) and 903 (Punjab) kg CO₂e/ha (Fig. 1, p. 3). In Punjab ~30% of farmers use zero or reduced tillage, 73% do not burn and 81% irrigate at most three times, so these are common practice; 86% overuse N, so cutting N is eligible. Without 'no burning' Punjab credits fall to 1.1 t/ha and farmer income to USD 16/ha. Bihar: 34 of 38 districts have zero tillage below 20%. No CA practice showed a significant yield penalty (pp. 3–6, Table 2).


## 5. Conclusions

1. Zero tillage of wheat (Punjab and Bihar) and reduced N overuse (Punjab) meet all conditions. 2. Credit prices must rise at least 60% (USD 16 vs USD 26/ha needed) for lasting adoption. 3. Government could fund a price floor from fertiliser or electricity subsidies; projects need strong MRV and extension (pp. 8–9).


## 6. Limitations

- Wheat season and two states only; not UP.
- Emissions modelled with CCAFS-MOT, not measured.
- Adoption measured as share of farmers, not area (the paper shows these are close).
- Eligibility depends on current laws and adoption; project costs only roughly deducted (10%).
