#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

The synthetic N fertiliser supply chain emitted about 1.13 Gt CO₂e in 2018 (1,129 ± 171 Mt), 10.6% of agricultural and 2.1% of global GHG emissions. Field use gave 58.6%, manufacture 38.8% and transport 2.6%. China, India, the USA and the EU28 gave 62%; India emitted 165.5 Mt CO₂e (14.7%) from 17.6 Mt N. Field N₂O estimates change by about 2× with the emission factor used. Cutting excess synthetic N use offers large, realistic mitigation (pp. 1–5, 7).


## 1. Background

Food systems cause about one-third of human GHG emissions, roughly 16.5 of 54 Gt CO₂e per year. FAO puts 2019 farm-gate emissions at 7.2 Gt CO₂e, with livestock 51.4% and synthetic N fertiliser 8.3% (p. 1). Synthetic N fertiliser emits at three stages: manufacture (ammonia made from fossil-derived hydrogen, releasing CO₂ and CH₄), transport, and field use, where soil microbes turn part of the N into N₂O, a gas with 265 times the warming effect of CO₂ over 100 years. Inventories place manufacture in the industry sector, so farm studies often miss it (pp. 1–2).


## 2. Aim of the study

To estimate GHG emissions from the whole synthetic N fertiliser chain, from manufacture to field, for each country in 2018; to derive better direct N₂O emission factors from a large field dataset; to test how emission-factor choice and uncertainty affect results; and to review trends in N use (p. 2).


## 3. What the authors did

- Country N use from FAOSTAT (107 Mt N in 2018); 96% (102 Mt) assigned to eight fertiliser product groups (pp. 8–9).
- Manufacture and transport emission factors from the Agri-footprint 6.0 dataset; a cross-check with Brentrup et al. factors gave 492 vs 438.5 Mt CO₂e (pp. 7, 9).
- Direct N₂O from the largest field-level dataset of soil N₂O measurements, giving country, regional and global EFs; indirect N₂O (volatilisation, leaching) and urea CO₂ from IPCC methods (pp. 1, 9–10).
- N₂O-N converted with 44/28 and GWP 265; uncertainty by Monte Carlo simulation (pp. 4, 10).

### 4.2 Countries

China (316.1 Mt CO₂e, 28.0%), India (165.5, 14.7%), the USA (115.5, 10.2%) and the EU28 (102.4, 9.1%) together emit 62%. China, India, the USA, the EU28 and Brazil use 68% of synthetic N. India has 13.6% of global direct soil N₂O (p. 3). Dividing India's Table 1 values gives about 9.4 kg CO₂e per kg N (our calculation).


### 4.3 Emission factors and uncertainty

- Global direct soil N₂O ranged from 380 to 666 Mt CO₂e (0.91–1.60 Mt N₂O-N) depending on the EF; IPCC Tier 1 gave 1.07 Mt N₂O-N (p. 3).
- The empirical country EF averaged 0.0085 ± 0.0036 (0.85%), against the IPCC default 0.0100 and the wet-dry disaggregated 0.0148 (p. 5).
- Total uncertainty (CV) was 15.1%; manufacture 8.5%, transport 13.5%, direct N₂O 42.2%, urea CO₂ 45.5%. Direct N₂O drives the overall uncertainty because it is both large and uncertain (pp. 4–5). 4.4 Trends and scenarios Per-capita N use rose from about 3 kg (1960) to 15 kg per person (2018). FAO reports a 44% rise in synthetic fertiliser N₂O from 1990 to 2019. N use could reach 260 Mt N/yr by 2050 under an intensive scenario or fall to 85 Mt N/yr with higher NUE; only the latter stays within the planetary boundary for N (pp. 5–6).

## 5. Conclusions

1. Reducing production and use of synthetic N fertiliser offers large and often realistic mitigation; manufacture is a major and overlooked share (pp. 1, 7).

2. Over-application is common; a 48% cut in cereal fertiliser use could keep yields, and better N management helps smallholders (p. 7). 3. Recycling manure, green manures and N-fixing crops, and eating less meat and dairy, reduce the need for synthetic N (pp. 7–8). 4. Better empirical EFs are needed, especially for Asia and Africa (p. 8).


## 6. Limitations

- Data refer to 2018 and are national, not spatially explicit (pp. 7–8).
- Field EFs are scarce for most countries and biased to North America and Europe (pp. 1, 7).
- About 4% of N with unknown product type is missing from manufacture and transport, so totals are underestimated (p. 9).
- Activity data have no uncertainty estimates (p. 7).
