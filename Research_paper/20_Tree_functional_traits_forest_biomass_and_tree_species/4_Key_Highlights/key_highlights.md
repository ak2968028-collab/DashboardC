# Key Highlights: Tree functional traits, forest biomass, and tree species diversity interact with site properties to drive forest soil carbon

**Authors:** Laurent Augusto, Antra Boča
**Journal / Year:** Nature Communications, vol. 13, article 1097 (2022) (2022)
**Official DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-28748-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-28748-0)

## Core Statistics & Results

| Metric | Finding / Significance |
| --- | --- |
| **454** | single-species stands |
| **48.5%** | SOC variance explained |
| **r = +0.61** | SOC vs biomass overyielding |
| **136** | sites worldwide |

## Important Findings & Key Takeaways

### 01. Traits and biomass
Explain about half (48.5%) of local SOC variation; Amax is the best predictor.

*(Reference: p. 2)*

### 02. Conservative species
Low-Amax species, often gymnosperms, store more SOC.

*(Reference: p. 3)*

### 03. Strongest
Boreal climates and acidic, poor, coarse soils.

*(Reference: p. 1)*

### 04. Tropics
Traits matter little; protect natural forests.

*(Reference: p. 5)*

### 05. Overyielding
Mixed forests store more SOC only if they grow more biomass (r = +0.61).

*(Reference: p. 5)*

### 06. Three levers
More biomass, more species, traits matched to site.

*(Reference: p. 1)*

### 07. Composition
Broadleaf at mid-latitudes or fertile soils; needleleaf at high latitudes or poor soils.

*(Reference: p. 5)*

### 08. Safer store
Soil carbon is less exposed to fire, drought and pests.

*(Reference: p. 2)*
