#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

This review gathers case studies on adding solar, wind, hydro, bioenergy and storage to electricity systems. Hybrid systems are more reliable and more resilient to climate change than single sources; PV/wind/hydro systems gave 2.11–5.46% more power under climate change. CO₂ savings rise with the renewable share, and a PV/wind/battery/diesel desalination system cut water cost by 69% and environmental impacts by 90%. Rural Indian cases include a Punjab PV/biogas microgrid at $0.0735/kWh. Renewables still have impacts (hydropower most), and poor design raises costs.


## 1. Background

Hydrocarbons  still  provide  over  80%  of  the  world's  energy.  Moving  to  electricity  is  seen  as  an improvement, but power generation is a major CO₂ source and renewables also have impacts (p. 1381). By the end of 2020 the world had 2,799 GW of renewable capacity: hydropower 1,211 GW (43%), wind 733 GW (26%), solar 714 GW (26%), bioenergy 127 GW and geothermal 14 GW (p. 1382).


## 2. Aim of the study

To review the integration of renewable energy into the electricity sector from social, environmental and economic perspectives, including hybrid systems, water desalination and climate resilience.


## 3. What the authors did

A narrative review of published studies on solar PV, wind, hydropower, bioenergy, geothermal and battery storage, single and hybrid. It compiles tables of optimal hybrid designs, renewable fractions, costs of energy and CO₂ emissions from individual case studies; no new data or pooled analysis.


## 4. Main findings


### 4.1 Social impacts

An extra 1 GWh a year of renewable power could offer 3.5 jobs; renewables employed 9.8 million people in 2016. Ram et al. (2020) project jobs from PV, battery storage, wind, hydropower and bioenergy; the abstract cites 504,000 jobs in 2030 and 4.18 million in 2050, but the body text lists five figures for different years 'respectively', so the pairing is unclear (p. 1385). Less firewood use benefits women's health.

### 4.3 Environmental trade-offs

Wind turbines embody 1,664–2,074 t CO₂e per MW. PV panels carry life cycle impacts such as toxicity and acidification. Hydropower is the most harmful to the environment and aquatic ecosystems; biomass and wind have minor impacts (pp. 1385–1387).


### 4.4 Desalination and climate resilience

A PV/wind/battery system backed by a diesel generator cut water cost by 69% and environmental impacts by 90% vs fossil-based units; for coastal Tamil Nadu villages, water cost $4.57/m³ (p. 1387). Under climate change, PV/wind/hydro systems raised power generation by 2.11–5.46% and guarantee rates by 3.74–71.61% (p. 1381; table p. 1396).


## 5. Conclusions

1. Hybrid systems are more reliable and better at withstanding climate impacts than single-source systems. 2. Raising the renewable share lowers net present costs, fuel use and pollution, in line with energy policies.

3. Large or poorly designed systems raise installation costs, so technical and financial evaluation is needed first. 4. A standard method to evaluate energy complementarity is lacking (pp. 1410–1411).


## 6. Limitations

- Numbers come from individual case studies with different sites, models and assumptions; they are not pooled.
- Some figures in the text are garbled (e.g. the jobs list; investment given as 'million' dollars), so check original sources.
- No emission-saving formula is provided.
- Only a few rural Indian cases; none for the Varuna basin.
