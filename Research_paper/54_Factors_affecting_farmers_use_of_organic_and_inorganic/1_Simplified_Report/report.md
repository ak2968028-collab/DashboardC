#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Using a 2013 survey of 2,528 households in Haryana, Bihar, the Nepal Terai and Bangladesh, the authors model what drives urea, DAP and manure use on rice and wheat and who overuses nitrogen. 33.6% of rice fields got more than 120 kg N/ha and 18.5% of wheat fields more than 140 kg N/ha. Overuse is highest in Haryana and lowest in Nepal; larger, wealthier, irrigated and more educated households overuse more, while training reduces overuse. Manure use is declining everywhere. The authors call for training, extension and nudges on nutrient-use efficiency.


## 1. Background

Fertiliser N is a leading cause of agricultural N₂O. In Punjab and Haryana N use rose from 2–8 kg N/ha in the 1960s to more than 160–180 kg N/ha in 2017, and many farmers do not know recommended rates. Earlier work found a wheat yield penalty above 140 kg N/ha and a rice plateau above 100 kg N/ha (pp. 51481, 51483).


## 2. Aim of the study

To examine socio-economic and geographic factors affecting inorganic and organic fertiliser use on rice and wheat in South Asia, and the factors behind N overuse (p. 51480).


## 3. What the authors did

Multistage sampling chose 38 villages (14 Bangladesh, 12 Bihar, 13 Haryana, 12 Nepal) in 2013. Total N = urea × 46% + DAP × 18% + manure × 0.5%. Multiple regression explained urea and DAP rates, Heckman two-step models manure adoption and amount, and a probit model N overuse (rice > 120, wheat > 140 kg N/ha; results similar at 100 and 120) (pp. 51482–51483, 51491).

## 5. Conclusions

1. Wealth, gender, education, migration, market access, training and off-farm income shape fertiliser use. 2. Where overuse is routine, training, education and behavioural nudges are needed; few farmers know the harms of overuse. 3. Declining manure use needs extension and scale-appropriate machinery to cut labour and cost (pp. 51492–51493).


## 6. Limitations

- Cross-sectional data from 2013; fertiliser amounts farmer-reported.
- Manure N assumed (0.5%); overuse thresholds are single cut-offs.
- One district per Indian state; no Uttar Pradesh site.
- N₂O is not estimated directly.
