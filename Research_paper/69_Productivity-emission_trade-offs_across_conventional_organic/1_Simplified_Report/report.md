#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

On 27 rice–wheat farms in Kaithal, Kurukshetra and Karnal (Haryana), 9 each under conventional, organic and natural farming, emissions for 2023 and 2024 were estimated with the Cool Farm Tool. Compared with conventional farming, rice emissions were about 32% lower under both organic and natural farming, with yield losses of 18% and 9%. In wheat, emission cuts were small (9.4% and 7.2%) while yields fell 16% and 40%. Natural farming gave the best balance in rice; organic did better in wheat.


## 1. Background

Rice-based and nitrogen-intensive cropping are the main sources of agricultural GHG in India, through CH₄ from flooded fields and N₂O from fertilised soils. Organic and natural farming may cut emissions, but lower yields can raise emissions per kg of grain, so productivity and emissions must be judged together.


## 2. Aim of the study

To compare GHG emissions and yields under conventional, organic and natural farming in the rice–wheat system of north-west India.


## 3. What the authors did

Twenty-seven farms (9 per system, at least 5 years under the system) were selected across three districts in one agro-climatic zone. Farmer interviews, yield records and 0–15 cm soil samples were collected for 2023 and 2024. Emissions were estimated with the Cool Farm Tool from activity data on fertiliser, residues, tillage, irrigation and energy. Natural farming inputs were represented through management proxies because the tool cannot model microbial inputs.

## 5. Conclusions

1. Conventional farming maximises yield but has the highest emissions. 2. Natural farming gives the best productivity–emission balance in rice; organic farming does better in wheat. 3. Mitigation should be crop- and region-specific, combining residue management, less tillage, optimised N and water-saving irrigation.


## 6. Limitations

- Emissions are modelled with the Cool Farm Tool, not measured.
- Only two years and 27 farms; observational design.
- Several reported numbers conflict (district ranking, figure values, abstract percentages).
- Haryana, not eastern UP.
