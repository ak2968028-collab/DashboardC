#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

In 2022 fossil CO₂ emissions were 9.9 ± 0.5 GtC (+0.9%), back at the 2019 level, and land-use change added 1.2 ± 0.7 GtC, a total of 11.1 GtC (40.7 GtCO₂). Of this, 4.6 GtC stayed in the atmosphere, the ocean took 2.8 GtC and the land 3.8 GtC. Fossil emissions were projected to rise a further 1.1% in 2023, with CO₂ reaching 419.3 ppm, 51% above pre-industrial. India's fossil emissions grew fastest among the big emitters (+3.5% per year over 2013–2022), but its share of fossil CO₂ since 1850 is 3%. 75 GtC remain for a 50% chance of 1.5 °C, about 7 years at 2023 emissions.


## 1. Background

Knowing how much CO₂ humans emit and where it goes (atmosphere, ocean, land) is needed to understand the carbon cycle, support climate policy and project future climate change (p. 5303–5304). The Global Carbon Budget is the Global Carbon Project's annual update. The 2023 edition gives final data for 2022, a projection for 2023, cumulative emissions since 1750 and 1850, and an updated remaining carbon budget (p. 5305).


## 2. Aim of the study

To describe and synthesise the data sets and methods that quantify the five major components of the global carbon budget and their uncertainties, and to document changes in methods and data since the previous edition (p. 5303–5304).


## 3. What the authors did

Fossil CO₂ emissions (EFOS) come from energy statistics and cement production data; land-use change emissions  (ELUC,  mainly  deforestation)  from  land-use  data  and  three  bookkeeping  models;  the atmospheric growth rate (GATM) from direct CO₂ measurements; the ocean sink (SOCEAN) from ocean biogeochemistry models and fCO₂-based data products; and the land sink (SLAND) from dynamic global vegetation  models.  Atmospheric  inversions,  atmospheric  oxygen  measurements  and  Earth  system models give extra lines of evidence on the land and ocean sinks. The budget imbalance BIM = EFOS + ELUC − (GATM + SOCEAN + SLAND) measures imperfect data and understanding. Uncertainties are ±1σ (p. 5303–5304). Units: 1 ppm = 2.124 GtC and 1 GtC = 3.664 GtCO₂ (p. 5306).


## 4. Main findings

- In 2022, EFOS was 9.9 ± 0.5 GtC per year, +0.9% on 2021 and virtually equal to 2019; ELUC was 1.2 ± 0.7, for a total of 11.1 ± 0.8 GtC per year (40.7 ± 3.2 GtCO₂) (pp. 5304–5305).
- In 2022, GATM was 4.6 ± 0.2 GtC per year (2.18 ± 0.1 ppm), SOCEAN 2.8 ± 0.4 and SLAND 3.8 ± 0.8, with a budget imbalance of −0.1 GtC per year; CO₂ averaged 417.1 ± 0.1 ppm (p. 5304).
- Preliminary 2023: fossil CO₂ +1.1% (0.0% to 2.1%) to 10.0 GtC (36.8 GtCO₂), 1.4% above 2019; CO₂ 419.3 ppm, 51% above about 278 ppm in 1750; the land sink falls to about 2.9 GtC as El Niño emerges (pp. 5304–5305).
- Over 2013–2022, fossil CO₂ grew 0.5% per year; India +3.5%, China +1.6%, EU27 −1.7%, USA −1.0% per year (p. 5319). Fossil CO₂ fell in 18 countries, together about 20% of world fossil emissions (p. 5304).
- Cumulative fossil CO₂ 1850–2022: USA 115 GtC (24%), EU 80 GtC (17%), China 70 GtC (15%), India 15 GtC (3%) (p. 5319).
- Land use: 1.3 ± 0.7 GtC per year over 2013–2022; deforestation about 1.9 GtC per year, of which re-/afforestation and forestry offset 1.3 GtC (p. 5304).
- Cumulative human CO₂ emissions 1850–2022: 695 ± 70 GtC, 70% since 1960 (p. 5327).
- Remaining carbon budget from January 2024 (50% likelihood): 75, 175 and 315 GtC (275, 625, 1150 GtCO₂) for 1.5, 1.7 and 2 °C, about 7, 15 and 28 years at 2023 emissions (pp. 5304, 5345).

## 5. Conclusions

The mean and trend of the budget components are consistently estimated over 1959–2022 with a near- zero overall imbalance, although discrepancies of up to about 1 GtC per year remain for annual to semi- decadal variability (p. 5304). Year-to-year variability in the land sink, about 1 GtC per year, dominates changes in atmospheric CO₂, so small annual changes in emissions are hard to detect (p. 5305). Even net zero by 2050 would emit 150 GtC, well above the 75 GtC left for 1.5 °C (p. 5345).


## 6. Limitations

- Global and national data only; no district or village values.
- CO₂ only; CH₄ and N₂O are covered by papers 2 and 11.
- 2023 figures are projections and every edition revises earlier years, so use the latest edition when citing numbers.
- Land-use change emissions remain the most uncertain flux; values are in GtC (× 3.664 for GtCO₂).
