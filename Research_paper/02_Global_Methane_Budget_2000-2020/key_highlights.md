## Important Findings & Key Takeaways

### 01. Global emissions
575 Tg CH₄/yr (553–586) over 2010–2019 from atmospheric inversions; 369 Tg (~65%) from direct human sources.

*(Reference: p. 1875)*

### 02. Bottom-up is higher
669 Tg CH₄/yr (512–849), 16% (94 Tg) more than top-down; for the first time the two ranges overlap.

*(Reference: pp. 1875–1876)*

### 03. Rising emissions
2020 reached 608 Tg CH₄/yr, 12% above the 2000s; since 2012 human emissions track no-policy scenarios (SSP5, SSP3).

*(Reference: p. 1875)*

### 04. Agriculture and waste lead
228 Tg/yr top-down and 211 Tg/yr bottom-up; fossil fuels 115–120; biomass and biofuel burning 27–28.

*(Reference: p. 1876, Table 3)*

### 05. Livestock is the largest single source
Enteric fermentation and manure: 112 Tg/yr, about one third of global anthropogenic CH₄.

*(Reference: p. 1889, Table 3)*

### 06. Rice paddies
32 Tg/yr; periodic drainage and aeration is one of the most promising mitigation options.

*(Reference: p. 1889, Table 3)*

### 07. Tropics dominate
Tropics 64%, mid-latitudes 32%, high northern latitudes 4% of emissions.

*(Reference: p. 1916)*

### 08. South Asia
52 Tg/yr (43–60, top-down), third after Southeast Asia (63) and China (57); agriculture and waste 39 Tg/yr; livestock 20 Tg/yr, the highest of any region.

*(Reference: pp. 1917–1919, Table 7)*

### 09. Natural sources
Wetlands + inland freshwaters 248 Tg/yr (159–369) bottom-up, with ~23 Tg/yr possible double counting; still the largest uncertainty.

*(Reference: p. 1875)*

### 10. OH sink
Tropospheric loss 563 Tg/yr with ~25% uncertainty; total sink 633 (bottom-up) and 554 Tg/yr (top-down).

*(Reference: p. 1876)*

### 11. GWP of methane
GWP-100: 27 (microbial CH₄) and 29.8 (fossil CH₄); GWP-20: 79.7 and 82.5.

*(Reference: p. 1876 (Forster et al. 2021))*
