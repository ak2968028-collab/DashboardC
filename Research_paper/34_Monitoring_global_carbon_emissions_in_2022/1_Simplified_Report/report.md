#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Global CO₂ emissions from fossil fuels and cement reached 36.1 ± 0.3 GtCO₂ in 2022, 1.5% more than in 2021 and 2.0% more than in 2019, so the COVID dip was short-lived and no peak is in sight. Power and industry gave two-thirds of the total. China's emissions fell for the first time (−1.5%), while India's rose 7%. At this pace the remaining 1.5 °C carbon budget could be used up within 2–7 years.


## 1. Background

COVID-19 caused a record fall in global CO₂ emissions in 2020 (−5.4%, 1.9 GtCO₂), but emissions rebounded by 6.3% (2.1 GtCO₂) in 2021. With little time left to meet the 1.5 °C or 2 °C goals, detailed, near-real-time tracking of emissions is crucial (p. 205).


## 2. Aim of the study

To present and analyse daily, national and sector-specific CO₂ emissions for 2022 using near-real-time data from the Carbon Monitor project, and to track the remaining carbon budget.


## 3. What the authors did

They used Carbon Monitor near-real-time data on fossil fuel combustion and cement production to estimate daily CO₂ emissions by sector and country for 2022, compared them with 2019–2021 and with the Global Carbon Project and IEA, and calculated how much of the IPCC and newer (Lamboll et al.) carbon budgets 2022 emissions used, including land-use change emissions.


## 4. Main findings


### 4.1 Global total

Emissions were 35.3 GtCO₂ in 2019, 33.4 in 2020, 35.5 in 2021 and 36.1 ± 0.3 in 2022: +2.0%, +7.9% and +1.5% relative to those years. The 1.5% growth (0.9–2.6%) agrees with the GCP (1.0 ± 0.9%) and the IEA (<1%).

### 4.3 Countries

The top five emitters produced 23.3 GtCO₂ (65% of the total). China's emissions fell 1.5%, its first reduction, perhaps because of the extended zero-COVID policy, but remained 5.6% above 2019. US and EU emissions rose 3.2% and 0.5%. India's emissions grew 7% (7.9% above 2019), on pace to pass the EU as the third largest emitter in 2023. Russia's fell 1.8%.

## 5. Conclusions

1. Global emissions continue to rise despite efforts to cut fossil energy use; the peak has not yet been reached. 2. The 1.5 °C budget could be exhausted within 2–7 years (67% likelihood). 3. Power, transport and industry need deeper decarbonisation and more renewable energy.

4. Countries must act now on net-zero pledges, with international cooperation; near-real-time monitoring shows early whether mitigation works.


## 6. Limitations

- Covers fossil fuel and cement CO₂ (land-use change is added only for the budget countdown); no CH₄, N₂O or F-gases.
- Near-real-time data are preliminary and are revised later.
- National and sector totals only; no village or district values.
