## Important Findings & Key Takeaways

### 01. Fossil-dominated power
Coal 35.1%, gas 23.4%, oil 2.8% of 2020 electricity; hydro 16%, nuclear 10.1%, wind 5.9%, solar 3.2%.

### 02. Power and emissions
Electricity generation causes about 26% of total GHG emissions; energy about two-thirds of CO₂.

*(Reference: pp. 2, 5)*

### 03. Three technological changes
Save energy on the demand side, generate efficiently, substitute fossil fuels with renewables and nuclear.

### 04. Keep existing plants efficient
Non-renewable plants still help reduce and stabilise costs during the transition.

*(Reference: p. 1)*

### 05. Five dimensions
Technical, environmental, economic, social and political-institutional sustainability.

### 06. Storage and resilience
A resilient grid with advanced storage is needed to absorb variable renewables.

*(Reference: p. 1)*

### 07. Smart grids
Two-way flows, variable pricing and peak shaving; ~10% of power is lost in transmission and distribution.

*(Reference: pp. 32, 38)*

### 08. Finance for rural renewables
Solar PV, micro-hydro, wind pumps, biodigesters and biogas need financing for low-income users.

*(Reference: p. 31)*

### 09. Three technology routes
Conventional mitigation, carbon capture and storage, radiation management.

*(Reference: pp. 1, 39)*

### 10. Policy and collaboration
Supportive policy, regulation, institutions and local and international cooperation.

*(Reference: pp. 2, 39)*
