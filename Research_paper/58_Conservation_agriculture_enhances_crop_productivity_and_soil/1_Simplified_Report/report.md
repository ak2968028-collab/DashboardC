#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Two conservation agriculture trials started in 2012 at Karnal (Haryana) and Samastipur (Bihar) compared conventional puddled rice-wheat with zero-till and raised-bed systems with residue retention and mungbean. CA raised system yield by up to 20.2% (Karnal) and 23.0% (Samastipur). Total organic carbon gains were concentrated in the top 5 cm, while labile fractions (POXC, coarse particulate organic matter) responded more strongly, so the authors propose them as early indicators of carbon sequestration.


## 1. Background

The Indo-Gangetic Plains (IGP) face land degradation and stagnant yields from intensive tillage, puddling and residue burning in the rice-wheat system. CA (minimal tillage, residue recycling, diversification) can build soil carbon, but total organic carbon (TOC) changes slowly and is hard to detect in experiments shorter than about five years. Labile fractions, such as permanganate-oxidisable carbon (POXC) and particulate organic matter (POM), may respond sooner (pp. 1–3).


## 2. Aim of the study

To evaluate the effect of resource-conserving practices on crop yield and TOC stocks at Karnal (western IGP) and Samastipur (middle IGP), to track changes in physical carbon fractions after 3 and 5 years, and to relate carbon stocks to labile fractions and crop residue inputs.


## 3. What the authors did

Trials started in 2012 (June at Karnal, November at Samastipur); the sites are about 1,300 km apart. Karnal had six treatments (K1–K6) and Samastipur four (S1–S4). Soil was sampled at 0–5 and 5–15 cm

after 3 and 5 years (2015 and 2017 at Karnal; 2016 and 2018 at Samastipur). TOC was measured by an improved chromic acid (Tyurin) method because the soils contain carbonates; POXC by KMnO₄ oxidation; and soil was wet-sieved into coarse POM (2,000–250 μm), fine POM (250–53 μm) and an organo-mineral fraction (< 53 μm). ## 4. Main findings


### 4.1 Yields

At Karnal, five-year mean system yield ranged from 11.9 Mg/ha (K1) to  14.3 Mg/ha (K6, +20.2%). Treatments with residue and mungbean (K2, K4, K6) did best. At Samastipur, S2 (15.0 Mg/ha) and S4 (13.7 Mg/ha) gave the highest system yields; S2 was 23.0% above S1. Raised-bed maize at Samastipur yielded less than conventional rice (Table 3, p. 9).


### 4.2 Carbon and nitrogen stocks

At Karnal, TOC stock at 0–5 cm in K4 and K6 was 17% and 11% higher than K1 in 2015, and 17% and 20% higher in 2017; over 0–15 cm the gain was 8–13%. At Samastipur, 0–5 cm TOC was highest in S2 (+24%) and S3 (+26%) in 2016 and in S2 (+20%) in 2018; over 0–15 cm S3 was highest (+11% in 2016, +8% in 2018). Below 5 cm, Samastipur stocks did not change significantly (p. 9). ### 4.3 Labile carbon and fractions

- POXC stock showed no treatment differences at Karnal in 2015; in 2017 it was 37.0% higher in K4 than K1 (0–5 cm). At Samastipur in 2018, S2 had 93.8% more POXC than S1.
- Residue increased coarse POM mass: +78.8% (K4) and +79.7% (K6) over K1 in 2015; K4 reached 3.90× K1 by 2017. K4's cPOM carbon was 3.42 Mg C/ha at 0–5 cm and 4.94 Mg C/ha at 0–15 cm (3.53× K1).
- At Samastipur, S2 had 3.19× (2016) and 2.41× (2018) the cPOM mass of S1. Carbon stocks correlated with crop residue carbon input (Table 10).

## 5. Conclusions

1. CA practices (no-till, residue management, crop diversification) significantly promote carbon sequestration, but outcomes depend on study duration, site and the fraction measured. 2. Three years was not enough to detect changes in soil carbon and nitrogen fractionation at Karnal; it was at Samastipur. 3. Coarse POM carbon and nitrogen, POXC and POC are useful early indicators of short-term CA effects; practices should be tailored to local soil and climate.


## 6. Limitations

- Short-term (3–5 years) trials at two research-managed sites.
- Soil sampled only to 15 cm; no greenhouse gas measurements.
- Abstract, Results and Discussion give somewhat different accounts of the 3-year carbon results.
