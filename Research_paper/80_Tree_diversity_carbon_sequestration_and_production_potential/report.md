#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Traditional rice-based agroforestry was surveyed in nine Uttarkashi villages across three elevation bands. Tree carbon rose from 30.49 to 34.43 Mg C/ha with elevation, and net tree sequestration reached about


### 9.13 Mg CO₂e/ha/yr. Soils under trees had higher SOC and lower bulk density than sole rice. But rice

grain yield was 14–17% lower under trees at every elevation, so carbon gains come with a food trade-off.


## 1. Background

Agroforestry offers social and ecological benefits, and traditional systems are common in the Garhwal Himalaya. Their carbon, soil and crop effects across elevations were not well known.


## 2. Aim of the study

To evaluate tree diversity, carbon sequestration, soil carbon, oxygen production and rice productivity under traditional agroforestry at different elevations.


## 3. What the authors did

Three villages in each of three elevation bands (800–1200, 1200–1600, 1600–2000 m) were sampled with 0.04 ha quadrats. Tree carbon = biomass × 0.50; yearly sequestration = CO₂e divided by tree age reported by farmers. Soil was sampled at three depths, and rice yield was measured in 1 × 1 m plots in agroforestry and sole rice fields.


## 4. Main findings

Table A. Tree carbon and rice grain yield by elevation Elevation Tree carbon (Mg C/ha) Rice, agroforestry (q/ha) Rice, sole (q/ha) 800–1200 m 30.49 17.49 21.05 1200–1600 m 33.80 16.63 19.31 1600–2000 m 34.43 11.48 13.71

- Tree diversity was highest at 1200–1600 m (Shannon 1.29, Simpson 0.69).
- Total oxygen production was highest at 1600–2000 m (91.79 Mg/ha).
- SOC reached 3.67% under agroforestry vs 2.73% in sole rice (upper band, 0–15 cm).
- Agroforestry soils had lower bulk density and higher pH. NOTE ON THE NUMBERS The 34.43 Mg/ha is carbon in trees only, not soil. Table 2 prints the above-ground total as 24.71, but the text gives 26.71, which is the value that sums to 34.43.

## 5. Conclusions

1. Tree density, diversity, carbon and oxygen vary with elevation. 2. Traditional agroforestry improves soil carbon and structure. 3. Rice yield is lower under trees, so management must balance ecological and economic needs.


## 6. Limitations

- Hill terraces with different species from the plains.
- Tree ages, used for yearly rates, came from farmers' recall.
- One season of rice yield.
