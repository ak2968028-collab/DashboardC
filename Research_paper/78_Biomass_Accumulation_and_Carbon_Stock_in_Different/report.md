#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Eight tree species planted at 4 × 4 m in 2007 with wheat were sampled destructively in the Himalayan foothills. Eucalyptus stored the most carbon in trees (43.85 Mg C/ha, 160.5 Mg CO₂/ha) and soil (37.5 Mg C/ha to 30 cm), followed by poplar (41.33 and 30.75). Tree carbon ranged from 30.0 to 43.85 Mg C/ha. Stems held 77–81% of tree biomass. Several abstract figures (leaf and root leaders, 45.33 Mg C/ha) conflict with the tables, so table values are used here.


## 1. Background

Agroforestry stores much more carbon than sole cropping; India's average potential is cited as 25 t C/ha over 96 Mha. Little was known about carbon in each tree component and in intercrops (p. 1083).


## 2. Aim of the study

To quantify the biomass of each component of agroforestry systems and the carbon stock in biomass and soil.


## 3. What the authors did

Three trees of each of eight species (Populus deltoides, Toona ciliata, Eucalyptus tereticornis, Albizia procera, Melia azedarach, Terminalia bellirica, Anthocephalus cadamba, Sapindus mukorosi) were felled, separated and weighed, with roots dug out. Wheat biomass and soil carbon (0–30 cm) were measured, and data were analysed by ANOVA.

Albizia's 'highest root biomass' (14.01 Mg/ha) is inconsistent with its total and root carbon. Stems were 77–81% of tree biomass; the '~50%' is a value cited from other studies.

## 5. Conclusions

1. Biomass and carbon are concentrated in the stem, then root, branch and leaf. 2. Eucalyptus and poplar systems store the most carbon in trees and soil. 3. Trees on farmland with crops suit biomass production and carbon storage.


## 6. Limitations

- Himalayan foothills, not the Gangetic plain; one tree age.
- Only three trees per species; no treeless control for soil carbon.
- Abstract and text figures conflict with the tables in several places.
