#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

An eight-year trial (2014–2022) at ICAR-CSSRI Karnal compared farmers' rice-wheat practice with six conservation agriculture (CA) scenarios using zero tillage, residue retention and diversified rotations. On average CA cut net global warming potential by 47.2%, raised surface soil C stock by 49.6%, system yield by 8.5% and profit by 23.0%. Maize-based rotations gave the best all-round result (+20.7% yield, +40.3% profit, −55.6% GWP).


## 1. Background

The rice-wheat system covers  13.5 million ha in South Asia and underpins food security and rural livelihoods. Intensive tillage, flooding and residue burning have depleted soil organic matter, slowed yield growth and raised greenhouse gas emissions (pp. 1–2). Conservation agriculture combines (a) reduced or zero tillage with residue cover, (b) crop diversification such as replacing rice with an upland crop, and (c) legumes in cereal rotations. The authors note a lack of medium- to long-term studies that look at soil quality, GHG mitigation and carbon balance together.


## 2. Aim of the study

To develop resilient, climate-smart cropping systems within the rice-wheat system and test their effect on crop productivity, economic returns, soil quality, nutrient balances and GHG emissions over eight years.


## 3. What the authors did

The trial ran from 2014 to 2022 at the ICAR-Central Soil Salinity Research Institute farm, Karnal, Haryana (semi-arid subtropical; average rainfall 670 mm, 70–80% in June–September; silty loam, OC 0.56%, pH 8.02). ### 4.1 Soil quality

CA scenarios had lower bulk density (range 1.34–1.52 Mg/m³ at 0–15 cm, highest in Sc1 and lowest in Sc5), better aggregation (water-stable aggregates +24.5% and mean weight diameter +118.7% at the surface) and faster infiltration. Surface organic C was 63.7% higher and C stock 49.9% higher (the

abstract and Table 6 give 49.6%). Available N, P and K rose by 10.2%, 28.6% and 21.8%. Earthworm density rose several-fold, reaching 333 per m² in Sc5.


### 4.2 Greenhouse gases

Flooded rice with residue burning (Sc1) emitted the most methane, 1972 kg CO₂-eq/ha in the rice season and 2299 kg CO₂-eq/ha for the system. CA scenarios had higher N₂O (1428–2211 vs 1041 kg CO₂-eq/ha), but lower methane and energy emissions: wet-season emissions from energy and inputs fell from 2853 kg CO₂-eq/ha in Sc1 by up to 72% in Sc5–Sc7. Net GWP fell by 47.2% on average.


### 4.3 Yield and economics

System yield ranged from 11.93 to 14.57 Mg/ha (rice equivalent) and returns from US$6616 to 9595/ha; both were highest in Sc5 and lowest in Sc1. Note that the Results text says yield and returns of CA rose 'approximately 9%', while Table 6 gives +8.5% for yield and +23.0% for profit.


## 5. Conclusions

1. CA practices and diversified rotations can reverse falling productivity, lower returns, soil degradation and rising environmental impacts in north-west India's rice-wheat system. 2. Maize-based rotations gave the highest overall benefits; rice-based CA gave the largest carbon-stock gain; legume rotations gave the largest GWP cut and the most negative N balance. 3. Wide adoption needs policy: carbon credit payments and guaranteed markets with supportive prices.


## 6. Limitations

- One research station in the western IGP; the Varuna basin (eastern IGP) has higher rainfall and smaller farms.
- Scenarios were changed after four years, so the eight-year effects mix two designs.
- Results are percent changes against farmers' practice, not annual sequestration rates; soil measured only to 30 cm.
- Shifting from rice to maize depends on procurement and prices.
