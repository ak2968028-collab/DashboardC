#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

IPCC reports come only every 5–10 years, so this group updates the key AR6 climate indicators each year with the same methods. For 2014–2023, observed warming was 1.19 °C and all of it was human-induced (1.19 [1.0 to 1.4] °C). Human-induced warming reached 1.31 °C in 2023 and is rising at 0.26 °C per decade, driven by GHG emissions at a high of 53 ± 5.4 GtCO₂e per year and weaker aerosol cooling. Only about 200 GtCO₂ of the 1.5 °C budget (50% chance) remained at the start of 2024.


## 1. Background

IPCC assessments are the trusted source of evidence for UNFCCC negotiations, but successive reports come out 5–10 years apart, leaving an information gap. AR6 used data up to 2019. The Indicators of Global Climate Change (IGCC) project gives annual updates; this is the second (pp. 2626–2627).


## 2. Aim of the study

To update, to the end of 2023, the indicators that link cause to effect: GHG and short-lived climate forcer emissions, GHG concentrations, effective radiative forcing (ERF), Earth's energy imbalance, surface temperature, human-induced warming, the remaining carbon budget and land maximum temperature.


## 3. What the authors did

They followed IPCC AR6 WGI (and WGIII Ch. 2) methods as closely as possible. CO₂e uses AR6 GWP100 values. Human-induced warming was estimated with the three AR6 attribution methods: the Global Warming Index (GWI), regularised optimal fingerprinting (ROF) and kriging for climate change (KCC) (p. 2639). Remaining carbon budgets were updated with the MAGICC emulator, AR6 WGIII scenarios and the new warming estimate (p. 2644). All data are open on Zenodo.

### 4.2 Concentrations, forcing and energy imbalance

In 2023, CO₂ was 419.3 ppm, CH₄ 1922.5 ppb and N₂O 336.9 ppb. Total human ERF was 2.79 [1.78 to 3.61] W m⁻² relative to 1750 (AR6: 2.72 W m⁻² for 2019). The Earth's energy imbalance averaged 0.96 W m⁻² over 2010–2023, up from 0.79 W m⁻² for 2006–2018 (Table 10, p. 2648). NOTE ON THE SOURCE The paper gives the 2023 ERF upper bound as 3.61 W m⁻² in Sect. 4 (p. 2633) but 3.60 W m⁻² in Table 10 (p. 2648).


### 4.3 Warming

Observed warming for 2014–2023 was 1.19 [1.06 to 1.30] °C and human-induced warming 1.19 [1.0 to 1.4] °C. In 2023 human-induced warming was 1.31 [1.1 to 1.7] °C, below the observed record of 1.43 [1.32 to 1.53] °C, which points to a large role for El Niño. Human-induced warming rose at 0.26 [0.2 to 0.4] °C per decade (Table 6, p. 2642; p. 2644). Land maximum temperatures reached 1.81 °C above pre- industrial in 2014–2023 (p. 2646).

## 5. Conclusions

1. This is a critical decade: human-induced warming rates are at their highest, and 1.5 °C may be reached within about 10 years. 2. Emissions are at a long-term high, but there are signs that their growth has slowed compared with the 2000s. 3. Rapid and stringent emission cuts could halve warming rates over the next 20 years. 4. Annual updates traceable to IPCC methods can track whether the indicators change direction (pp. 2649–2650).


## 6. Limitations

- Global indicators only; no regional or national values.
- Large uncertainties remain in aerosol forcing and land-use CO₂ (±70%).
- Natural CH₄ changes are excluded while wildfire aerosol is included, which is inconsistent (p. 2649).
- 2023 non-CO₂ emissions were not yet available.
