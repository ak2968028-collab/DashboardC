#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A discrete choice experiment with 503 rural households in Odisha (surveyed 2011–12) found that households will pay a premium for biogas but not for an improved biomass (rocket) stove (mean premium −INR 2,768). The biogas premium depends on experience: INR 7,348 after a good experience, 5,744 after malfunctions and 2,837 with no experience. Households valued smoke reduction (INR 4,785) and fuel efficiency (INR 3,074). The authors recommend adding trials, quality products, maintenance and customer service to subsidies.


## 1. Background

About 2.8 billion people lacked clean cooking access in 2018, and almost one-third of Indians still cook mainly with polluting fuels. Biogas can meet rural cooking needs while reducing fuel and fertiliser costs, pressure on forests, and emissions of PM₂.₅ and methane. Many past biogas programmes produced plants that broke down, so households differ in what they know about the technology.


## 2. Aim of the study

To measure household preferences for biogas and an improved biomass cookstove and their attributes, and to test how past biogas experience and risk and time preferences shape willingness to pay (WTP).


## 3. What the authors did

They surveyed  503 households in 42 villages in 8 districts of Odisha between November 2011 and February  2012,  stratified  by  biogas  experience  (good:  plant  never  broke;  bad:  broken  or  past malfunctions; none). In a discrete choice experiment, respondents chose between a biogas system, a rocket stove and the traditional chulha, described by price (4,000, 7,000 or 10,000 INR), maintenance assistance, smoke and fuel need. A random-parameters logit in WTP space was estimated. Risk aversion

and impatience were measured with hypothetical payment questions; 64% of respondents were classed as risk-averse.

Risk-averse and impatient respondents had lower WTP for the rocket stove; for biogas the gap appeared only among households without experience. The authors note that the WTP distributions overlap in most cases, so 'it is unclear to what extent time and risk preferences matter'. Biogas users tended to be lower- and middle- income households with more livestock.


## 5. Conclusions

1. Households value smoke reduction and fuel efficiency and will pay a premium for biogas, but not for the improved biomass stove. 2. Negative experiences (malfunctions) reduce the value placed on biogas. 3. Biogas uptake could be improved by complementing subsidies with technology trials, good-quality products, maintenance and customer service to reduce uncertainty.


## 6. Limitations

- Data are from 2011–12; prices, LPG access and schemes have changed since.
- Stated preferences, not observed purchases; most respondents were men.
- One state (Odisha); no emission measurements.
