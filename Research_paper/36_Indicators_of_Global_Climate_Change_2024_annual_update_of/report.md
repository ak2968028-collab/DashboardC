#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

This third annual update of the IPCC AR6 climate indicators covers data to 2024. For 2015–2024, observed warming was 1.24 °C, of which 1.22 [1.0 to 1.5] °C was human-induced. Human-induced warming reached 1.36 °C in 2024 and is rising at 0.27 °C per decade, driven by record GHG emissions (53.6 ± 5.2 GtCO₂e per year, 2014–2023) and weaker aerosol cooling. The energy imbalance is 25% above AR6 and sea level is rising faster. A 2026 corrigendum puts the remaining 1.5 °C budget (50%) at 160 GtCO₂ from 2025, about four years of current emissions.


## 1. Background

IPCC reports come out 5–10 years apart, while the climate is changing fast. The Indicators of Global Climate Change (IGCC) project gives policymakers annual updates traceable to IPCC AR6 methods; this is its third edition (pp. 2641–2642).


## 2. Aim of the study

To update, to the end of 2024, the indicators of the state of the climate: GHG and short-lived climate forcer emissions, concentrations, effective radiative forcing (ERF), Earth's energy imbalance, surface temperature, human-induced warming, the remaining carbon budget and temperature extremes, adding land precipitation and sea-level rise this year.


## 3. What the authors did

They followed IPCC AR6 WGI methods as closely as possible. CO₂e uses AR6 GWP100 values, and the WGIII emissions convention was compared with national inventories. Human-induced warming used the three AR6 attribution methods; remaining carbon budgets used TCRE with emulators. All data are open on Zenodo. ### 4.2 Concentrations, forcing and energy imbalance

In 2024, CO₂ was 422.8 ppm, CH₄ 1929.7 ppb and N₂O 337.9 ppb. Total human ERF was 2.97 [2.05 to 3.77] W m⁻² relative to 1750 (AR6: 2.72 W m⁻² for 2019; p. 2651). The energy imbalance averaged 0.99 W m⁻² over 2012–2024, 25% above the AR6 value for 2006–2018 (Table 11, p. 2667).


### 4.3 Warming, extremes and sea level

Observed warming for 2015–2024 was 1.24 [1.11 to 1.35] °C and human-induced warming 1.22 [1.0 to 1.5] °C. In 2024 observed warming was 1.52 [1.39 to 1.65] °C and human-induced 1.36 [1.1 to 1.7] °C (Table 6, p. 2660). The human-induced rate was 0.27 [0.2 to 0.4] °C per decade (Table 7, p. 2662). Land maximum temperatures reached 1.90 °C in 2015–2024. Sea level rose 228.0 mm from 1901 to 2024; since 1993 the rate is 3.46 mm per year (Table 10, p. 2666).

## 5. Conclusions

1. This is a critical decade: human-induced warming rates are at their highest, and 1.5 °C may be reached in around 5 years. 2. Earth's energy imbalance has risen to about 1.0 W m⁻², 25% above AR6, with committed sea-level rise to follow. 3. Emissions are at a long-term high but their growth has slowed; rapid cuts could halve warming rates over 20 years. 4. Annual updates depend on open, well-supported monitoring networks (pp. 2669–2670).


## 6. Limitations

- Global indicators only; no regional or national values.
- Large uncertainties in aerosol forcing and land-use CO₂ (±70%).
- One of the three AR6 attribution methods is diverging (Table 11).
- Budget values changed after publication (2026 corrigendum); 2024 non-CO₂ emissions were not yet available.
