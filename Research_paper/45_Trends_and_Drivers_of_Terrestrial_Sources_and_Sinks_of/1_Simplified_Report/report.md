#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

TRENDY is an annual ensemble of Dynamic Global Vegetation Models (17 in TRENDY-v11) run with a common protocol to supply land fluxes to the Global Carbon Budget. Factorial runs split land carbon change into effects of CO₂, climate and land use. Over 2012–2021, rising CO₂ gave a sink of 3.8 ± 0.8 Pg C/yr and climate reduced it by 0.58. Land-use change emitted 1.6 ± 0.5 Pg C/yr, leaving a net land sink of 1.7 ± 0.6 Pg C/yr. Forests and semi-arid lands contribute about equally to the natural sink. The largest net uptake is outside the tropics.


## 1. Background

Land ecosystems absorb almost one third of human CO₂ emissions, and land-use change adds emissions. Regular regional and global estimates are needed for Paris Agreement stocktakes. TRENDY has run annually since 2013 and supports the Global Carbon Budget and RECCAP-2 (pp. 2–3).


## 2. Aim of the study

To document the TRENDY-v11 protocol and forcing data, benchmark the models against observations, and present land carbon sources and sinks for 2012–2021 (p. 3).


## 3. What the authors did

Seventeen DGVMs ran at about 0.5° from 1700 to 2021 with CRUJRA climate and LUH2-GCB land use. Four factorial simulations (S0 control, S1 CO₂, S2 CO₂ + climate, S3 all forcings) attribute changes to drivers. Models enter the budget only if they reach steady state, show a net land sink in the 1990s and 2000s, and a land-use source in the 1990s. Performance was benchmarked in ILAMB (pp. 9–12).


## 4. Main findings


### 4.1 Benchmarking

The ensemble matches spatial biomass patterns reasonably but overestimates tropical and boreal biomass and underestimates mid-latitude biomass. It performs well for soil carbon (overestimating high latitudes), GPP, runoff and evapotranspiration (pp. 12–13).

### 4.3 Ecosystems

Forests and semi-arid ecosystems contribute about equally to the mean and trend of the natural sink; semi-arid ecosystems dominate interannual variability, which follows ENSO. The tropical forest share of the trend declined from 1982–2001 to 2002–2021 (pp. 1, 15). NOTE DGVM land-use emissions include the 'loss of additional sink capacity', about 40% of present-day DGVM land-use emissions, so they are not directly comparable with bookkeeping or national inventory numbers (p. 10).


## 5. Conclusions

1. TRENDY gives regularly updated, process-based estimates of land sources and sinks for the Global Carbon Budget and regional assessments. 2. The net land sink results from large opposing effects of CO₂ fertilisation and land-use change. 3. Future work: weight models by benchmark skill, use multiple climate and national land-use data sets, and add near-real-time assessments (pp. 17–19).


## 6. Limitations

- One climate data set and one land-use reconstruction drive all models.
- The CO₂ fertilisation effect is highly uncertain across models; few models represent demography, drought mortality or land management.
- Coarse (0.5°) resolution; known problems with China's land-use data.
