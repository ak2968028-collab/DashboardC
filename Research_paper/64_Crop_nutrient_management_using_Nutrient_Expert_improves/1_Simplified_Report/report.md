#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Across 1,594 paired on-farm trials in Punjab, Haryana and Bihar (2013–14 to 2016–17), Nutrient Expert (NE) fertiliser advice cut N use by about 18% and modelled global warming potential by about 2.5% in rice and 12–20% in wheat compared with farmers' practice, while raising yields by about 4–12%. More than 80% of farmers gained yield and income, although NE's extra potash raised fertiliser cost for about half of them. ## 1. Background

Fertiliser N is a major source of N₂O. India uses 14% of the world's fertiliser but has one of the lowest nutrient use efficiencies, and rice and wheat use half of India's fertiliser. Nutrient Expert (NE) is a decision-support tool that gives field-specific nutrient advice with or without a soil test, but it had not been tested on many farms.


## 2. Aim of the study

To test whether NE-based site-specific nutrient management raises yield and income and lowers GHG emissions in rice and wheat compared with farmers' own fertiliser practice (FFP) across the Indo- Gangetic Plains (IGP).


## 3. What the authors did

Over four seasons (2013–14 to 2016–17) they ran 1,594 paired trials: 1,094 in Haryana, 245 in Punjab and 251 in Bihar (730 rice, 864 wheat). Each farm had an NE plot and an FFP plot. Yield was measured from three 3 m² quadrats per plot. GHG emissions were estimated per plot with the CCAFS Mitigation Options Tool, not measured in the field.


## 4. Main findings

- NE cut N use (about 10% in East IGP and 25% in West IGP) and P₂O₅ use (except West IGP rice), but increased K₂O.
- NE out-yielded FFP in all 135 East IGP rice pairs, 78% of 116 East IGP wheat pairs, 83% of 595 West IGP rice pairs and 91% of 748 West IGP wheat pairs.
- GWP fell by about 2.5% in rice and 12–20% in wheat; cuts were larger in West IGP and where farmers used more N and P₂O₅.

## 5. Conclusions

1. NE-based nutrient management raises yields and income for most farmers while cutting N use and GHG emissions. 2. Benefits depend on crop, agro-ecology, soil and the farmer's current fertiliser rate. 3. NE is practical for smallholders where soil testing is hard, and could be scaled through extension and the Soil Health Card Scheme.


## 6. Limitations

- Emissions are modelled with CCAFS-MOT, not measured.
- No trials in Uttar Pradesh; Bihar is the closest site to the Varuna basin.
- National totals assume trial results apply to all rice and wheat land and the upper wheat value.
- Extra K₂O use has cost and import implications that were not studied.
