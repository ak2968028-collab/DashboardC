#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

In 2021 fossil CO₂ emissions rebounded by 5.1% to 9.9 ± 0.5 GtC, the 2019 level, and land-use change added 1.1 ± 0.7 GtC, a total of 10.9 ± 0.8 GtC (40.0 GtCO₂). Of this, 5.2 GtC stayed in the atmosphere, the ocean took 2.9 GtC and the land 3.5 GtC. Fossil emissions were projected to rise a further 1.0% in 2022, with CO₂ reaching 417.2 ppm, 51% above pre-industrial. India's fossil emissions grew fastest among the big emitters (+3.8% per year over 2012–2021). 105 GtC remain for a 50% chance of 1.5 °C, about 9 years at 2022 emissions and 32 GtC less than in the IPCC AR6 assessment.


## 1. Background

Knowing how much CO₂ humans emit and where it goes (atmosphere, ocean, land) is needed to understand the carbon cycle, support climate policy and project future climate change (p. 4813–4814). The Global Carbon Budget is the Global Carbon Project's annual update. The 2022 edition gives final data for 2021, a projection for 2022, cumulative emissions since 1750 and 1850, and an updated remaining carbon budget (p. 4815).


## 2. Aim of the study

To describe and synthesise the data sets and methods that quantify the five major components of the global carbon budget and their uncertainties, and to document changes in methods and data since the previous edition (p. 4813–4814).


## 3. What the authors did

Fossil CO₂ emissions (EFOS) come from energy statistics and cement production data; land-use change emissions  (ELUC,  mainly  deforestation)  from  land-use  data  and  three  bookkeeping  models;  the atmospheric growth rate (GATM) from direct CO₂ measurements; the ocean sink (SOCEAN) from ocean biogeochemistry models and fCO₂-based data products; and the land sink (SLAND) from dynamic global vegetation models. Atmospheric inversions, which close the global carbon balance by design, are assessed as an independent check (p. 4815). The budget imbalance BIM = EFOS + ELUC − (GATM + SOCEAN + SLAND) measures imperfect data and understanding. Uncertainties are ±1σ (p. 4813–4814). Units: 1 ppm = 2.124 GtC and 1 GtC = 3.664 GtCO₂ (p. 4816).


## 4. Main findings

- In 2021, EFOS was 9.9 ± 0.5 GtC per year (10.1 ± 0.5 without the cement carbonation sink), +5.1% on 2020; ELUC was 1.1 ± 0.7, for a total of 10.9 ± 0.8 GtC per year (40.0 ± 2.9 GtCO₂) (p. 4813).
- In 2021, GATM was 5.2 ± 0.2 GtC per year (2.5 ± 0.1 ppm), SOCEAN 2.9 ± 0.4 and SLAND 3.5 ± 0.9, with a budget imbalance of −0.6 GtC per year; CO₂ averaged 414.71 ± 0.1 ppm (pp. 4813–4814).
- Preliminary 2022: fossil CO₂ +1.0% (0.1% to 1.9%) to 10.0 GtC (36.6 GtCO₂), slightly above 2019; total 11.1 GtC (40.5 GtCO₂); CO₂ 417.2 ppm, 51% above pre-industrial (p. 4814).
- Over 2012–2021, fossil CO₂ grew 0.5% per year; India +3.8%, China +1.5%, EU27 −1.8%, USA −1.1% per year (p. 4826). Fossil CO₂ fell in 24 countries, together about a quarter of world fossil emissions (p. 4814).
- Cumulative fossil CO₂ 1850–2021: USA 115 GtC (24%), EU 80 GtC (17%), China 70 GtC (14%); no share is given for India (p. 4826).
- Land use: 1.2 ± 0.7 GtC per year over 2012–2021; deforestation 1.8 ± 0.4 GtC per year, of which afforestation, reafforestation and forestry offset 0.9 ± 0.3 GtC (p. 4814).
- The ocean sink trend since 2010 differs by a factor of 3 between data products (+0.7 GtC per year per decade) and models (+0.2) (p. 4814).
- Remaining carbon budget from January 2023 (50% likelihood): 105, 200 and 335 GtC (380, 730, 1230 GtCO₂) for 1.5, 1.7 and 2 °C, about 9, 18 and 30 years at 2022 emissions; net zero by 2050 needs cuts of about 0.4 GtC (1.4 GtCO₂) per year (pp. 4814, 4847).

## 5. Conclusions

The mean and trend of the budget components are consistently estimated over 1959–2021, but discrepancies of up to 1 GtC per year persist for annual to semi-decadal variability (p. 4814). Year-to-year variability in the land sink, about 1 GtC per year, makes small annual changes in emissions hard to detect in atmospheric CO₂ (p. 4815). Reaching zero CO₂ by 2050 needs a linear cut of about 0.4 GtC per year, comparable to the 2020 COVID-19 drop, which shows the scale of action needed (p. 4814).


## 6. Limitations

- Global and national data only; no district or village values.
- CO₂ only; CH₄ and N₂O are covered by papers 2 and 11.
- 2022 figures are projections and every edition revises earlier years, so use the latest edition when citing numbers.
- Land-use change emissions remain the most uncertain flux; values are in GtC (× 3.664 for GtCO₂).
