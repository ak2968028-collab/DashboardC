#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Over two wheat seasons at Karnal, zero-till wheat sown with a Happy Seeder into full rice residue raised soil microbes (47.9–60.4% at harvest), soil moisture (13.4–23.6%) and grain yield (9.8–11.3%) compared with burning or removing the straw. Final SOC reached 0.42% (from 0.35%) under Happy Seeder, against 0.32–0.33% with burning or removal. Zero-till options had the lowest costs, with net returns of ₹43,160– 57,080/ha and B:C of 1.52–1.70.


## 1. Background

The north-west IGP rice–wheat system covers 4.1 Mha and produces about 34 Mt of rice residue. Combine harvesting leaves loose straw, and the window before wheat sowing is short, so farmers burn it, harming soil microbes and organic matter (p. 1).


## 2. Aim of the study

To assess how Happy Seeder-based and other rice residue options affect soil microbes, enzymes, organic carbon, moisture and wheat productivity in sandy loam soils of Haryana.


## 3. What the authors did

Ten treatments were tested in 2018–19 and 2019–20: conventional wheat after burning, removal or incorporation, and zero-till wheat with anchored stubble, partial or full burning, or full residue sown by

Happy Seeder (Table 1). Soil (0–15 cm) was analysed for microbes, enzymes, pH, SOC and moisture; yield and economics were recorded.

## 5. Conclusions

1. Planting method and residue management strongly affect soil microbes, SOC and enzyme activity. 2. Zero-till wheat with Happy Seeder in full residue gives higher yield and soil health than burning. 3. Rice residue should be managed by sowing wheat with suitable zero-till machinery.


## 6. Limitations

- One site and two seasons.
- No greenhouse gas measurements; SOC is a concentration only.
- Yields are given in a figure, not a table.
