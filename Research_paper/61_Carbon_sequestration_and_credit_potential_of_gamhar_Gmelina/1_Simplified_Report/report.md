#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A two-year trial at Ranchi compared gamhar (Gmelina arborea) agroforestry with arhar, cowpea-mustard or greengram-mustard intercrops against sole trees and sole crops. Because the trees were only 1–2 years old, sole greengram-mustard gave the highest total carbon stock (7.33 t C/ha, pooled) and credits (26.9 t CO₂/ha), while sole gamhar gave the lowest (0.84 t C/ha). Gamhar + arhar built the most soil carbon. The authors expect gamhar systems to sequester much more as the trees grow.


## 1. Background

The paper cites India's GHG emissions rising from 746.5 Mt CO₂e in 1970 to 3375 Mt CO₂e in 2018, and India's net-zero 2070 target (Panchamrit). Agroforestry is promoted under the National Agroforestry Policy (2014), the Sub-Mission on Agroforestry and the Trees Outside Forests in India (TOFI) programme, which aims to add 2.8 Mha of tree cover and 420 Mt CO₂-eq of sequestration (pp. 1–2).


## 2. Aim of the study

To assess the carbon sequestration and carbon credit potential of gamhar-based agroforestry compared with sole gamhar and sole crops.


## 3. What the authors did

The trial was at Birsa Agricultural University, Kanke, Ranchi (625 m altitude; Eastern Plateau and Hill Region) in 2016–17 and 2017–18. Seven treatments (gamhar + arhar; gamhar + cowpea-mustard; gamhar + greengram-mustard; sole gamhar; sole arhar; sole cowpea-mustard; sole greengram-mustard) were laid out in a randomised block design with five replications. Five-month-old gamhar seedlings were planted in June 2016 at 8 × 2.5 m (500 trees/ha). Tree stem volume came from the quarter-girth formula and wood density (530 kg/m³); root biomass was assumed to be 25% of above-ground biomass. Crop biomass was measured by uprooting plants per m². Soil carbon (0–30 cm) = SOC% × depth × bulk density. Carbon was converted to CO₂ to give credits (1 credit = 1 t CO₂), valued at about ₹1,500 (US$20). Emissions from inputs were estimated with the ICAR- IIFSR GHG tool, and net emission = emission − sequestration.

- Trees: gamhar biomass was slightly higher with intercrops (e.g. +16.5% with arhar in 2016–17), but treatment differences were not significant. Stem made up 50–60% of tree biomass, leaves 19.2– 20%, branches 10.7–12.7%, roots about 20% (by assumption).
- Crops: biomass was lower under trees; sole greengram-mustard had 88.7% more crop carbon than gamhar + arhar (pooled).
- Soil: gamhar + arhar had the highest soil carbon stock, 348.5% above sole gamhar (pooled).
- Net emission: lowest in sole greengram-mustard (760.7% lower than sole gamhar, pooled); negative values mean sequestration exceeded emission. READ WITH CARE The 348.5% gain for gamhar + arhar is for soil carbon, not total carbon. The total stocks (0.84–7.33 t C/ha) are far below a typical 0–30 cm soil carbon stock, so the soil values appear to be increments rather than full stocks (our assessment).

## 5. Conclusions

1. In the first two years, sole greengram-mustard gave the highest total biomass, carbon stock, sequestration, credits and carbon value. 2. Carbon emission from tree components was low and declined each year. 3. Gamhar is fast-growing, so gamhar agroforestry should have high long-term sequestration and support India's tree-cover and net-zero goals.


## 6. Limitations

- Trees were only one to two years old, so agroforestry benefits are understated.
- One site in Jharkhand; root biomass assumed rather than measured.
- Credit price (US$20) is an assumption and varies by market.
