#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Using 3,395 observations from 178 publications and a random forest model, the authors mapped iron- bound organic carbon (OC-Fe) in the top 30 cm of soil. The global stock is 233 Pg C, about 33% of topsoil SOC. Soils hold on average 33% of their modelled iron-bound maximum; croplands are lowest at 25.5%, with a 143 Pg C deficit. Croplands also have the lowest mean SOC (17.2 g/kg). Soil pH is the top control. The authors call for mineral factors in soil carbon models.


## 1. Background

Soil organic carbon (SOC) is the largest continuously cycling carbon pool on land. It includes particulate organic carbon (POC), partly decomposed plant material with a short residence time, and mineral- associated organic carbon (MAOC), protected by bonds with minerals (p. 1). Fe-Al oxides are the primary minerals bound to SOC (p. 4), but the global amount of iron-bound carbon (OC-Fe), its maximum capacity (OC-Femax) and its controls were unclear (p. 2).


## 2. Aim of the study

To map Fe-Al oxides and use them to estimate global stocks of SOC, MAOC and OC-Fe; to find the factors controlling OC-Fe; and to estimate the contribution of OC-Fe to SOC and MAOC (p. 2).


## 3. What the authors did

The authors compiled 3,395 observations from 178 publications that reported SOC and at least one mineral factor, with OC-Fe extracted by the citrate-bicarbonate-dithionite method; pot, lab and model studies were excluded (p. 7). A random forest model (R² = 0.90 in five-fold cross-validation) predicted OC-Fe and OC-Femax for 0–5, 5–15 and 15–30 cm across croplands, forests, grasslands, shrublands and wetlands (130.1 million km²; tundra and deserts excluded) (pp. 3–5).


## 4. Main findings

- The global topsoil OC-Fe stock is 232.9 Pg C, 33.5 ± 15.6% of SOC (pp. 3, 6).
- Mean SOC was highest in wetlands (117.9 g/kg) and permafrost (117.6 g/kg) and lowest in croplands (17.2 ± 19.7 g/kg); in topsoil, croplands were also lowest (19.5 ± 10.8 g/kg) (p. 2).
- Soils hold on average 33.0% of their modelled OC-Fe maximum, leaving a global deficit of 995 Pg C (Table 1).
- Large deficits occur at the equator and mid-latitudes (p. 1).
- Soil pH is the most important control: lower pH raises Fe and Al in solution and increases SOC adsorption onto Fe oxides. Oxalate-extractable Fe, clay and silt, and total N follow (pp. 3, 5). ## 5. Conclusions

Iron oxides play an important role in stabilising soil carbon worldwide. Mineral factors should be added to soil carbon models to improve predictions. The global maps of OC-Fe and OC-Femax can guide soil carbon sequestration, although OC-Fe extraction methods carry uncertainty and OC-Femax comes from an idealised model (pp. 1, 7).


## 6. Limitations

- Sparse data in northern Canada, central and western Australia, Russia and western Europe (p. 7).
- Few subsoil observations, so only 0–30 cm was mapped with confidence (p. 7).
- Part of the variation remains unexplained (p. 7).
- No management practices were tested; the maps are global and not specific to Indian alluvial soils.
