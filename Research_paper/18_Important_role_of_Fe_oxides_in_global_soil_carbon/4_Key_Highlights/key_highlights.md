## Important Findings & Key Takeaways

### 01. OC-Fe
233 Pg C in 0–30 cm, 33 ± 15% of SOC.

*(Reference: p. 1)*

### 02. Data
3,395 observations from 178 publications; random forest R² = 0.90.

*(Reference: pp. 2–3)*

### 03. Croplands
Lowest SOC (17.2 g/kg; 19.5 in topsoil) and lowest OC-Fe saturation (25.5%).

*(Reference: pp. 2, 4)*

### 04. Highest SOC
Wetlands and permafrost (about 118 g/kg).

*(Reference: p. 2)*

### 05. Deficits
Largest at the equator and mid-latitudes; 995 Pg C globally.

*(Reference: pp. 1, 5)*

### 06. pH
Top factor: lower pH increases binding of SOC to Fe oxides.

*(Reference: pp. 3, 5)*

### 07. Minerals
Fe-Al oxides are the primary minerals binding SOC.

*(Reference: p. 4)*

### 08. Models
Include mineral factors; extraction methods remain uncertain.

*(Reference: p. 1)*
