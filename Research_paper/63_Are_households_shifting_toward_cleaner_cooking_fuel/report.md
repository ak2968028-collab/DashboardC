#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Using three rounds of India's National Family Health Survey (2005–06, 2015–16, 2019–21; 1,276,508 household records), the authors show a clear shift to LPG: rural gas use rose from 11% to 39% and urban from 63% to 86%. Still, 48% of rural households used wood as their main fuel in 2019–21. A multinomial logit model finds that education, wealth, urban location and female headship raise the odds of using LPG, while larger households use more straw and dung.


## 1. Background

Household cooking with firewood, dung, kerosene and straw harms health and adds to emissions. The 'energy ladder' idea says households move to LPG and electricity as they get richer. Little was known about how India's household fuel choices changed over the last two decades.


## 2. Aim of the study

To describe trends in household cooking fuel in India from 2005 to 2021 and identify the factors that drive the choice of fuel.


## 3. What the authors did

They pooled NFHS-3, NFHS-4 and NFHS-5 (1,276,508 households; 71% rural). Households average about five members; 84% of heads are male, with an average age of 48. The main cooking fuel (nine categories) was modelled with a multinomial logit, with wood as the base category, controlling for age, gender, education, household size, wealth, caste, rural location, survey round and state.

- LPG use by households with no education rose from 11% to 17% to 36%; with higher education it was 84%, 78% and 83%.
- In 2019–21, higher-educated rural households used about 60% LPG and 20% wood; urban ones above 90% and below 2%.
- More than 70% of the poorest households use firewood, almost unchanged over time.
- Compared with 2005–06, the 2019–21 odds ratios for LPG, electricity and biogas were 19.0, 17.2 and 5.1. ## 5. Conclusions

1. A large number of Indian households switched toward LPG and electricity between 2005 and 2021. 2. Rural, the poorest and uneducated households lag behind. 3. Policies for income growth and education can reduce household-energy emissions; LPG and electricity subsidies should target poor households.


## 6. Limitations

- Only the main cooking fuel is recorded, so fuel stacking is not captured.
- Repeated cross-sections, not a panel; associations, not causal effects.
- National analysis with no emission estimates.
