#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Protected areas were rarely set up for climate, and their carbon impact had not been measured globally. Using about 412 million lidar samples from NASA's GEDI mission, the authors estimate that protected areas hold 61.4 Gt of aboveground carbon, 26% of mapped woody carbon on about 11% of forest area. Compared with matched unprotected land, protection preserved an extra 9.65 Gt C, roughly one year of fossil emissions, mainly through avoided deforestation. Brazil's Amazon gives 36.6% of the signal.


## 1. Background

Global terrestrial aboveground carbon was about 308 Gt in 2010, with uptake of about 8 Gt CO₂ a year. Protected forests are thought to give about 27% of the net global sink, but past satellite biomass maps saturate in dense forests, so the carbon effect of protection was uncertain (pp. 1–2).


## 2. Aim of the study

To estimate aboveground carbon in protected areas and the additional carbon attributable to protected status (carbon effectiveness) within the GEDI domain (p. 2).


## 3. What the authors did

GEDI lidar (25 m footprints, 52°N–52°S) was aggregated to 1 km carbon density. Each protected pixel was matched to unprotected pixels similar in 2000 in climate, human pressure, land type and country; 2020 differences represent about 20 years of protection. Landsat forest loss and a year-2000 carbon map were used to test whether the gap came from avoided loss, faster growth, or siting (pp. 2, 7).


## 4. Main findings

Protected areas store 61.4 ± 0.31 Gt C, 26% of the 235 Gt mapped, on about 11% of the 16.2 Mkm² forested area. ## 5. Conclusions

1. Protected areas effectively prevent carbon emissions from deforestation and degradation and are an essential natural climate solution. 2. Maintaining and expanding PAs in high-carbon, high-loss forests is essential for carbon goals. 3. PAs also keep taller, denser canopies, giving biodiversity co-benefits; CBD targets support UNFCCC goals (pp. 5–7).


## 6. Limitations

- No data north of ~52°N; North America and Europe underestimated.
- Aboveground woody carbon only; no belowground carbon or future sequestration.
- No leakage analysis and no comparison of PA types or governance.
