#### IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A 15-year experiment (2006–07 to 2020–21) at Modipuram, UP compared four rice and five wheat establishment methods. Zero-till direct-seeded rice followed by Happy Seeder wheat with residue (ZTDSR-THSW + R) used the least energy (47,110 vs 65,830 MJ/ha for puddled rice + tilled wheat), had the highest energy use efficiency (13.6) and 53–54% lower GWP, with comparable system yield. The GWP cut comes mostly from dropping puddled rice, because methane was assumed negligible in direct- seeded rice; emissions were estimated from emission factors, not measured.


## 1. Background

Puddled transplanted rice (PTR) and conventionally tilled wheat (CTW) dominate the Indo-Gangetic rice– wheat system. They use much energy, emit GHGs and degrade soil. Conservation agriculture (CA), with zero tillage and residue retention, may save energy and cut emissions.


## 2. Aim of the study

To evaluate the long-term effects of rice and wheat establishment methods on yield, energy use, carbon indices and GHG emissions in the rice–wheat system.


## 3. What the authors did

At ICAR-IIFSR, Modipuram (upper IGP; farm under CA since 1998), a split–split plot experiment with three replicates ran from 2006–07 to 2020–21. Main plots were four rice methods (ZTDSR, drum-seeded DSDSR, machine-transplanted puddled PMTR, puddled PTR); sub-plots were five wheat methods (zero-till ZTW, Turbo Happy Seeder with residue THSW + R, bed planter BPW, rotary till RTW, conventional CTW). Energy budgets covered all inputs. GWP = N₂O × 298 + CH₄ × 34 + CO₂ was estimated with emission factors (Lal 2004 and others); CH₄ was counted only for puddled rice.


## 4. Main findings

Table A. Selected systems (Tables 3 and 4, 15-year means) System Energy (MJ/ha) Yield (kg/ha) GWP (kg CO₂e/ha) ZTDSR-THSW + R 47,110 10,744 5,377 ZTDSR-ZTW 47,838 10,715 5,354 DSDSR-CTW 57,949 10,555 6,041 PTR-THSW + R 58,373 11,298 11,310 PTR-CTW 65,830 11,083 11,688

- Fertiliser (27–37%), electricity (22–28.5%) and irrigation water (21–25%) were the largest energy inputs; irrigation was 45–53% of energy by operation.
- Direct-seeded rice with conventional wheat used 14–21% less energy than puddled rice with conventional wheat; ZTDSR-THSW + R used 28% less than PTR-CTW (computed).
- Zero-till combinations had 53–54% lower GWP than PTR-CTW and PMTR-CTW; wheat tillage alone changed GWP by under 5%.
- Interaction effects on yield and energy were not statistically significant. Neither matches the paper's own formulas (8,907 ÷ 2,293 ≈ 3.9), so these indices should not be reused.

## 5. Conclusions

1. ZTDSR with zero-till or Happy Seeder wheat was the most energy-efficient and lowest-GWP system. 2. System productivity stayed comparable despite slightly lower ZTDSR rice yields. 3. DSR plus zero-till wheat with residue management is a viable climate-smart pathway for the IGP.


## 6. Limitations

- Research farm in western UP, not farmers' fields in eastern UP.
- GHG emissions estimated with general emission factors, not measured; CH₄ from DSR assumed negligible.
- Carbon index values are internally inconsistent.
- Access to Happy Seeders and zero-till drills may be limited in villages.
