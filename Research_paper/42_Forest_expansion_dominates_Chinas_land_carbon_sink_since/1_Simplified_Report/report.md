## 1. Background

Land-use and cover-change (LUCC) emissions are among the most uncertain terms in the global carbon budget and caused about 25% of the historical rise in CO₂. Models use the LUH2 data set, built from FAO country reports of agricultural area and not constrained by forest area (p. 1).


## 2. Aim of the study

To build an improved LUCC database for China, use it to simulate carbon stock changes from 1900 to 2019 (focusing on 1980–2019), compare with MsTMIP and TRENDY, and attribute the sink to land use, climate, forest management, nitrogen deposition and CO₂ (p. 2).


## 3. What the authors did

They showed that FAO data inherited spurious cropland jumps from switching between three Chinese sources  (Agricultural  Yearbook  before  1996,  Land  and  Resources  Statistical  Yearbook  1996–2007, National Land and Resources Bulletin after 2007) with different survey methods and under-reporting incentives. They rebuilt yearly provincial cropland and forest areas benchmarked to the latest surveys

and national forest inventories, mapped them on grids, and ran DLEM at 0.5° with factorial experiments (pp. 2–5).


## 4. Main findings


### 4.1 Land-use data

Cropland grew 56 Mha since 1900 in the new data (LUH2-GCB: 60 Mha), but since 1980 it fell 14 Mha instead  of  rising  41  Mha.  Forest  area  rose  58  Mha  from  1900  to  2019,  while  other  databases underestimated forest expansion after 1980 by 38–102 Mha. FAO's 'land use' definition gave about 30 Mha too much forest in 1990 and 30 Mha too little expansion from 1990 to 2019 (pp. 2–3).


### 4.2 Carbon stocks

Total carbon fell 6.9 ± 0.6 Pg from 1900 to 1980 and rose 8.9 ± 0.8 Pg C from 1980 to 2019, mostly in biomass (p. 4). Forest expansion gave a sink of 0.21 Pg C/yr, while TRENDY shows almost none and MsTMIP a source. ### 4.3 Attribution

The abstract gives land-use change about 44% of the sink, climate 22.3%, nitrogen deposition 12.9% and CO₂ 8.1%. The factorial results give land-use change 50.3% (3.96 Pg C), climate 18.0% (1.41 Pg C), other factors 0.1–9.54%, and interactions 18.1% (pp. 4–5). ## 5. Conclusions

1. Biased LUCC data made earlier studies strongly underestimate China's land carbon sink. 2. Land-use change, mainly forest expansion, dominated the sink since 1980; CO₂, N deposition and climate contributed much less. 3. Reforestation can be an important mitigation pathway with biodiversity co-benefits, and policy for carbon neutrality should improve land and forest management (p. 6).


## 6. Limitations

- One process model (DLEM), though checked against MsTMIP and TRENDY and re-run with LUH2 data.
- Historical land-use reconstruction depends on survey records with known biases.
- China only, at national and provincial scale; attribution shares differ between abstract and results.
